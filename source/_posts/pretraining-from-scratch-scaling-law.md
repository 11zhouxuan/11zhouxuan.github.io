## 概述

本文记录了从零开始预训练一个 1.7B 参数语言模型的完整实验过程。我们使用 Qwen3-1.7B 的架构（随机初始化权重），在 FineWeb-Edu 数据集上训练，并拟合了 token scaling law。实验揭示了预训练过程中的关键现象，包括 loss 的 power law 衰减、不同 token 的学习难度差异，以及数据重复对模型能力的影响。

## Scaling Law 拟合

### 公式

我们拟合了 Chinchilla 风格的 token scaling law（固定模型大小 N=1.7B，变化数据量 D）：

$$L(D) = a \cdot D^{-b} + c$$

其中 $D$ 为训练 tokens 数量（单位：B tokens）。

### 拟合结果

使用 step 500 到 step 2500 的数据（1B-5.2B tokens）拟合，得到：

$$L(D) = 3.611 \cdot D^{-0.658} + 2.00$$

| 参数 | 值 | 含义 |
|------|------|------|
| $a$ | 3.611 | 缩放系数 |
| $b$ | 0.658 | 幂律指数（接近 Chinchilla 的 ~0.5） |
| $c$ | 2.00 | 不可约 loss（数据熵下限） |

### 外推验证

用前 5 个数据点拟合的公式预测后续趋势：

| Tokens | 实际 Val Loss | 预测 Val Loss | 误差 |
|--------|-------------|--------------|------|
| 6.3B | 3.05 | 3.08 | -0.03 |
| 8.4B | 2.90 | 2.89 | +0.01 |
| 10.5B | 2.81 | 2.77 | +0.04 |
| 11.5B | 2.77 | 2.72 | +0.05 |

外推误差 ±0.05，说明 power law 对本实验拟合良好。

|--------|--------------|-----------|
| 20B | 2.50 | 12x |
| 34B | 2.35 | 20x (Chinchilla optimal) |

## 关键发现

### 1. Val Loss 下降速率的 Power Law 衰减

每 500 步的 val loss 下降量（Δ）呈明显的递减趋势：

| 区间 | Δ Val Loss |
|------|-----------|
| step 0-500 | -6.91 |
| step 500-1000 | -1.08 |
| step 1000-1500 | -0.64 |
| step 2000-2500 | -0.21 |
| step 4000-4500 | -0.05 |
| step 7000-7500 | -0.02 |
| step 9000-9500 | -0.009 |

### 2. Train Loss ≈ Val Loss（无过拟合）

由于训练数据 98B tokens 全为 unique（1 epoch），train loss 和 val loss 始终接近（差距 < 0.05）。这符合信息论预测——当数据量远超模型记忆容量时，模型被迫泛化而非记忆。

### 3. 文档边界效应

通过 token-level loss 分析发现，EOS token 后（文档开头）的 loss 显著高于普通位置：

| 位置 | 平均 Loss |
|------|----------|
| EOS 后第一个 token | 6.46 |
| 普通位置 | 2.73 |
| 差距 | +3.73 |

模型在文档边界处缺乏上下文，预测能力显著下降。

### 4. Token 类型影响学习难度

| Token 类型 | 初始 Loss | 最终 Loss | 提升 |
|-----------|----------|----------|------|
| 数字 | 12.18 | 1.22 | 10.96 |
| 标点 | 12.24 | 1.52 | 10.72 |
| 普通文字 | 12.38 | 2.82 | 9.56 |

数字和标点因模式固定最容易学习，普通文字（含知识和语义）最难。

### 5. Bigram 条件概率与 Loss 的关系

通过分析 bigram 条件概率 $P(B|A)$ 与模型 loss 的关系：

| P(B\|A) 范围 | 平均 Loss | 含义 |
|---|---|---|
| 0-0.01 (几乎随机) | 4.10 | 模型需要长距离上下文 |
| 0.10-0.30 | 1.42 | bigram 有一定确定性 |
| 0.80-1.00 (几乎确定) | 中位 0.06 | 子词续写，几乎完美预测 |

相关系数：Corr(P(B|A), loss) = -0.30，说明模型学到的远不止 bigram 统计。

## 知识评估（PopQA Benchmark）

我们使用 PopQA 基准测试评估模型的事实知识记忆能力。PopQA 包含 14,267 个基于 Wikidata 的知识问答题，覆盖 occupation、director、author、country 等 16 种 property 类型，按实体流行度分层。

### 评估格式（官方）

采用 15-shot in-context learning，格式为 `"Q: {question} A: {answer}"`：

```
Q: Who was the composer of Now or Never? A: Billie Holiday

Q: What genre is Drôles de zèbres? A: comedy film

Q: In what city was Jun Murai born? A: Tokyo

... (15 examples)

Q: What is George Rankin's occupation? A:
```

### 评估结果

| 评估方式 | Overall | Very Low (<100) | Low (100-1K) | Medium (1K-10K) | High (>10K) |
|----------|---------|-----------------|--------------|-----------------|-------------|
| Zero-shot | 2.4% | 2.6% | 1.7% | 1.9% | 6.7% |
| 15-shot (官方格式) | 15.4% | 6.1% | 13.2% | 21.7% | 35.6% |

### 分析

1. **高频实体记忆更好**：流行度 >10K 的实体准确率 35.6%，而 <100 的只有 6.1%。这与训练数据中的曝光频率直接相关。

2. **训练数据曝光频率决定知识记忆**：我们统计了 PopQA 实体在 FineWeb-Edu 训练数据中的实际出现频率：
   - "法国首都巴黎"相关文本出现 ~41 万次 → 模型完美回答 ✓
   - "George Rankin" 出现 0 次 → 模型完全不知道 ✗
   - 500 个测试实体中 83% 在训练数据中从未出现

3. **Few-shot 的作用是格式引导而非知识注入**：zero-shot 到 15-shot 的提升（2.4% → 15.4%）主要来自模型学会了"短答案"的输出格式，而非获得了新知识。

4. **高频实体的常识测试**：对于训练数据中高频出现的常识（如各国首都、科学常数），模型表现优异：

| 知识点 | 训练数据曝光次数 | 正确？ |
|--------|-----------------|--------|
| 法国首都巴黎 | ~670K | ✓ |
| 水沸点 100°C | ~1.09M | ✓ |
| 光速 300,000 km/s | ~71K | ✓ |
| 金的化学符号 Au | ~4.4M | ✓ |
| George Rankin 是 politician | 0 | ✗ |

### 分析

1. **我们的模型 vs 同架构官方模型**：我们的 1.7B 模型（25B tokens 训练）val loss 2.576，而 Qwen3-1.7B-Base 为 2.395，差距 0.181。PopQA 上 15.4% vs 27.8%。这完全由训练 token 数量差异解释——官方模型训练了数万亿 tokens，见过了更多知识。

2. **知识量随模型规模增长但非线性**：从 0.6B 到 8B，PopQA 从 22.8% 增长到 37.0%。但 14B 反而略低于 8B (35.4% vs 37.0%)，说明知识记忆不完全由模型容量决定，训练数据配比和训练策略同样关键。

3. **代际进步**：同等参数规模下，Qwen2.5 显著优于 Qwen2（例如 7B: 2.004 vs 2.134），Qwen3.5 进一步优于 Qwen3（例如 ~4B: 2.229 vs 2.242），主要归因于训练数据质量和规模的持续提升。

4. **MoE 的参数效率**：Qwen3-30B-A3B 和 Qwen3.5-35B-A3B 仅 3B 活跃参数，val loss (1.947/1.934) 均优于 14B dense 模型。MoE 在推理效率（FLOPs）上有巨大优势——用 1/5 的计算量达到更好的效果。

5. **Scaling law 视角**：从 Qwen2.5 系列看，dense 模型 14B→32B 仅下降 0.064 (1.843→1.779)，边际收益递减明显。而 MoE 路线以低成本突破了这一瓶颈。

## 知识注入实验：Permutation-based Knowledge Injection

### 动机

我们的 baseline 模型在 PopQA 上只有 10.6%（随机 500 题评估），而 83% 的测试实体在训练数据中从未出现。能否通过少量定向训练快速注入这些知识？

### 方法

**核心思想**：将知识事实随机排列组合成训练样本，使模型无法通过记住"样本序列"来作弊，只能记住知识本身。

1. **知识库构建**：从 PopQA 全部 14,267 题中提取 14,221 条唯一知识三元组 (subject, property, object)
2. **多样化表述**：每条知识有 5 种 surface form（如 `"X was directed by Y"`, `"The director of X is Y"`, `"Y directed X"` 等）
3. **随机排列组合**：每个 8192-token 训练样本随机采样 ~680 条知识，随机排列顺序，每条随机选一种表述
4. **知识间用换行分隔**：模型在一个样本内看到多条无关知识，强迫学习每条知识本身
5. **生成 5000 个样本**（41M tokens），每条知识平均曝光 ~245 次
6. **混合通用数据防遗忘**：knowledge_ratio=0.9（90% 知识 + 10% FineWeb-Edu 通用数据）

### 训练配置

| 参数 | 值 |
|------|------|
| Base checkpoint | step_12000（25B tokens 预训练） |
| 知识数据 | 5000 samples × 8192 tokens = 41M tokens |
| 混合比例 | 90% 知识 + 10% 通用 |
| Learning rate | 5e-5 (cosine schedule) |
| Batch size | 4, grad_accum=4 |
| 总步数 | 5000 |

### 训练曲线

| Step | PopQA (Generate) | Val Loss | Train Loss |
|------|-----------------|----------|-----------|
| 0 | 14.8% | 2.562 | - |
| 1000 | 55.8% | 2.579 | 1.21 |
| 2000 | 61.6% | 2.582 | 1.15 |
| 3000 | **66.2%** | 2.592 | 1.11 |
| 4000 | 66.0% | 2.594 | 1.10 |
| 5000 | 63.6% | 2.621 | 1.05 |

> 注：上表的 PopQA 评估使用数据集前 500 题（全为 occupation 类型）。下面使用随机采样 500 题重新评估。

### 随机采样评估（覆盖全部 16 种 property 类型）

发现 PopQA 数据集按 property 排序，前 500 题全为 occupation。改为随机采样 500 题后：

| Model | Generate Acc | Perplexity Acc | Gap (记住但检索失败) |
|-------|-------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Ours + knowledge (1.7B)** | **43.6%** | **81.2%** | 38.6% |
| Qwen3-1.7B-Base | 16.2% | 45.8% | 30.2% |
| Qwen3-4B-Base | 20.2% | 53.8% | 34.6% |
| Qwen3-8B-Base | 26.4% | 59.8% | 34.4% |
| Qwen3-14B-Base | 29.8% | 62.4% | 33.8% |

其中 Perplexity Acc 的评估方式：给定问题和正确答案，计算答案部分的 loss，与 5 个同类错误答案比较——如果正确答案 loss 最低则判为"已存储"。

### 按 Property 类型分析

| Property | Our r90 Gen | Our r90 PPL | Qwen3-14B Gen | Qwen3-14B PPL |
|----------|------------|------------|---------------|---------------|
| country | 95.8% | 83.3% | 75.0% | 83.3% |
| religion | 100.0% | 100.0% | 14.3% | 100.0% |
| sport | 82.6% | 87.0% | 73.9% | 82.6% |
| occupation | 80.0% | 93.3% | 46.7% | 53.3% |
| capital | 77.8% | 100.0% | 70.4% | 100.0% |
| author | 47.3% | 90.9% | 30.9% | 65.5% |
| father | 43.5% | 82.6% | 39.1% | 91.3% |
| composer | 35.0% | 85.0% | 27.5% | 70.0% |
| screenwriter | 31.9% | 79.2% | 16.7% | 44.4% |
| director | 15.9% | 69.8% | 14.3% | 47.6% |
| producer | 22.2% | 73.3% | 13.3% | 42.2% |

### 关键结论

1. **定向知识注入远超通用预训练**：1.7B 模型用 41M tokens 的知识数据训练后，Perplexity 准确率 (81.2%) 远超训练了数万亿 tokens 的 14B 官方模型 (62.4%)。

2. **知识确实被存储了**：81.2% 的知识事实在 perplexity 评估中得到确认，说明 permutation 方法能有效将知识写入模型参数。

3. **检索是瓶颈而非存储**：38.6% 的知识"记住了但说不出来"——模型对正确答案赋予了最低 loss，但在 greedy decoding 时无法生成正确 token。这在所有模型规模上都存在（30-38%），是 few-shot QA 格式的固有限制。

4. **知识类型影响检索难度**：
   - 简单检索（答案是常见词）：country 95.8%, sport 82.6%, capital 77.8%
   - 困难检索（答案是 rare 人名）：director 15.9%, producer 22.2%
   - 但 perplexity 维度即使是 director 也达到了 69.8%

5. **通用能力基本保持**：Val loss 从 2.562 升至 2.621（+0.059），代价很小。

### 对照实验：基于官方 Qwen3-1.7B-Base 的知识注入

为了验证"检索 gap"是否源于 base model 的通用能力不足，我们在官方 Qwen3-1.7B-Base（训练了数万亿 tokens）上用完全相同的方法进行知识注入。

**训练曲线：**

| Step | PopQA (Generate, 前500题) | Val Loss |
|------|--------------------------|----------|
| 0 | 28.2% | 2.378 |
| 1000 | 94.8% | 2.383 |
| 2000 | 96.2% | 2.390 |

### 消融实验：Permutation vs 简单重复

为了验证 permutation 方法本身的贡献，我们设计了严格的对照实验。在相同条件下（官方 Qwen3-1.7B-Base，相同 token 总量，相同曝光次数）对比：

- **With Permutation**：随机排列 + 随机 surface form，每个 sample 都不同
- **No Permutation**：固定顺序 + 固定 surface form，每个 sample 完全相同（纯重复）

**随机 500 题评估结果：**

| 方法 | Generate Acc | Train Loss | Val Loss 变化 |
|------|------------|-----------|-------------|
| No Permutation | 37.8% | 0.30 | +0.025 |
| **With Permutation** | **86.2%** | 1.12 | +0.017 |

**按 Property 对比：**

| Property | No Permutation | With Permutation |
|----------|---------------|-----------------|
| director | 9.5% | **77.8%** |

**分析：**

1. **Permutation 带来了 +48.4% 的绝对提升**（37.8% → 86.2%），证明这不是锦上添花而是质的飞跃。

## LoRD：从大模型压缩参数来加速预训练

### 动机

从零预训练的一个根本浪费是：模型必须从随机初始化开始重新学习所有语言规律，而这些规律早已存在于开源的大模型权重里。能否把 Qwen3-8B 的参数"压缩"到 1.7B 规模，作为预训练的起点？

我们的方法称为 **LoRD (Low-Rank Distillation)**，思路借鉴 LoRA 但目标相反：LoRA 冻结原矩阵 $W$ 并加上低秩增量 $AB$；LoRD 则用 $AB$ 去**逼近** $W$，然后**彻底丢弃** $W$，只保留 $A, B$ 继续预训练。

每个线性层 $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ 替换为 $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ 和 $B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$：

$$W x \approx A B x$$

取 rank $r=384$，Qwen3-8B 的 252 个线性层从 6945.8M 参数压缩到 1047.5M（**15.1%**）。

### 参数量拆解

需要注意总参数量的构成，否则容易误判这个对比是否公平：

| 组件 | LoRD r384 | 从零训 1.7B |
|---|---|---|
| Transformer 主体 | **1047.5M** | **1409.3M** |
| embed_tokens | 622.3M | 311.2M (tied) |
| lm_head | 622.3M (未 tie) | — (tied) |
| norms | 0.3M | 0.3M |
| **总计** | **2.2925B** | **1.7206B** |

LoRD 的总量（2.29B）大于 1.7B，但差额几乎全部来自 embedding —— Qwen3-8B 的 hidden=4096（是 1.7B 的两倍）且不 tie word embeddings。**真正做计算的 Transformer 主体，LoRD 反而比 1.7B 少了 26%**。这对 LoRD 是加分项：用更小的主体达到了更好的效果。若把 embedding tie 起来，总量会降到 1.67B，比从零训还小。

（需要诚实指出：embedding 部分是完整继承 Qwen3-8B 的，未做任何压缩，这一块知识是"白送"的。）

### 初始化方式的对比：SVD vs 随机

先做一个 rank=1024 的小规模验证（单卡，2000 步）：

| 初始化 | Step 0 | Step 2000 |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| 随机初始化 | 18.61 | 6.39 |
| **SVD 初始化** | 11.68 | **2.98** |

SVD 初始化的优势不仅在起点，更在于**训练 2000 步后差距仍有 3.41**。这说明低秩分解确实保留了可利用的结构信息，而非仅仅提供了一个更好的起点。

### 关键问题：逐矩阵 SVD 是最优的吗？

朴素做法是对每个矩阵独立做 SVD，最小化**权重误差** $\|W - AB\|_F$。但这个目标是错的。模型真正关心的是**输出误差** $\|WX - ABX\|_F$，二者只在 $\mathbb{E}[XX^T] \propto I$ 时等价 —— 而 LLM 激活严重违反这个条件，存在著名的 **outlier channels**（少数输入维度幅值比其他大 10-100 倍，这也是 SmoothQuant/AWQ 系列量化工作的出发点）。

我们实测了 Qwen3-8B 的激活统计（8 个 batch，65K tokens）：layer 0 的 `down_proj` 输入通道 $\text{mean}|x_i|$ 从 0.0036 到 0.154，**跨度 42.9 倍**。plain SVD 把 rank 预算平摊到所有方向，等于把大量容量浪费在幅值只有 1/43 的通道上。

这个诊断也解释了 rank sweep 里最反直觉的现象：

| rank | 参数量 | 初始 val loss |
|------|--------|--------------|
| 4096 | 11.3B（**比原模型还多**） | 2.11 |
| 2048 | — | 9.71 |
| 1024 | — | 11.71 |
| 512 | — | 19.91 |
| 384 | 2.29B | 18.65 |

rank=4096 参数量比原模型（8B）还多，loss 却仍有 2.11（原模型约 1.6）。原因是 rank=4096 对 $4096 \times 4096$ 的方阵是无损的，误差全部来自 MLP 的 12288 维矩阵 —— 而那里正是 outlier 最严重的地方。

### Activation-aware SVD (ASVD)

修正方法是换变量。引入可逆矩阵 $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$：

$$WX = \underbrace{(WS)}_{\text{分解这个}} \underbrace{(S^{-1}X)}_{\text{被白化的激活}}$$

对 $WS$ 做 SVD 截断，得到

$$A = U_r \sqrt{\Sigma_r}, \qquad B = \sqrt{\Sigma_r} V_r^T S^{-1}$$

$S$ 有两种选择：

**变体 1 — 对角缩放**（原始 ASVD 论文）：$S = \text{diag}(s_i)$，$s_i = (\text{mean}|x_i|)^{\alpha}$。便宜，$S^{-1}$ 平凡，数值稳定。

**变体 2 — Cholesky 白化**（SVD-LLM / Swift-SVD）：$SS^T = \Sigma = \mathbb{E}[XX^T]$。此时 $S^{-1}X$ 的协方差为单位矩阵，SVD 截断在输出误差意义下**严格最优**（Eckart–Young 定理在白化坐标系下的应用）。代价是需要 $n \times n$ 的 Gram 矩阵及其三角求解。

### 实验结果（rank=384，零训练初始 val loss）

| 方法 | $\alpha$ | 初始 val loss | vs plain SVD |
|------|---------|--------------|-------------|
| plain SVD | — | 18.65 | — |
| diag-ASVD | 0.25 | 18.69 | +0.04 |
| diag-ASVD | 0.5（论文默认） | 10.83 | **−7.82** |
| diag-ASVD | 0.75 | 9.35 | **−9.30** |
| **diag-ASVD** | **1.0** | **8.91** | **−9.74** |
| diag-ASVD | 1.25 | 9.11 | −9.54 |
| diag-ASVD | 1.5 | 10.09 | −8.56 |
| diag-ASVD | 2.0 | 11.79 | −6.86 |
| **whiten-ASVD** | — | **8.64** | **−10.01** |

**分析：**

1. **同参数量、零训练，loss 从 18.65 降到 8.64（−54%）**。唯一的改动是把优化目标从权重误差换成输出误差，成本仅是 8-32 个 batch 的前向统计。这说明 plain SVD 在这个任务上**优化了错误的目标**。

2. **$\alpha$ 存在阈值效应而非平滑权衡**：$\alpha=0.25$ 时 loss 为 18.69，与完全不缩放（18.65）几乎无差别；但 $\alpha=0.5$ 骤降到 10.83。缩放力度不足时收益为零，超过阈值后收益巨大。

3. **最优 $\alpha=1.0$，高于论文默认的 0.5**。$\alpha=1.0$ 对应 $S = \text{diag}(\text{mean}|x_i|)$，即完全按激活幅值线性加权 —— 一个更简洁的形式。超过 1.0 后单调变差（过度补偿）。

4. **whiten (8.64) 略优于最佳 diag (8.91)**，与理论预期一致（whiten 是严格最优解，diag 是其对角近似）。但差距仅 0.27，而计算代价高得多：whiten 需要 29GB 内存存 Gram 矩阵、45 分钟 CPU 统计、每层 fp64 Cholesky + 三角求解。**diag with $\alpha=1.0$ 是实践中的性价比之选。**

5. **ASVD 主动放弃权重拟合精度**。在合成实验中（256×256 压到 rank 64，植入 8 个 30× outlier 通道）：

| 方法 | 输出误差 | 权重误差 |
|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | 0.6167 | **0.6123** |
| diag-ASVD | 0.1189 | 0.6242 |
| whiten-ASVD | **0.1152** | 0.6339 |

ASVD 的权重误差**反而更大** —— 它主动牺牲权重拟合去换输出精度。这正是它该做的事。

### 实现细节

- **激活统计的去重**：Qwen3 block 内 q/k/v_proj 共享同一输入（input_layernorm 输出），gate/up_proj 共享另一个（post_attention_layernorm 输出）。只对每组的代表层累积统计，把 whiten 模式的内存从 58GB 降到 29GB。
- **统计阶段只走 transformer 主体**：跳过 lm_head，否则会 materialize 一个 $8192 \times 151936$ 的 logits 张量（fp32 约 5GB）。
- **分块计算 eval loss**：把 lm_head 按 1024 token 分块，避免 cross_entropy 对完整 logits 升 fp32 导致 OOM。
- **显存隔离**：由于主训练在同一张卡上运行，用 `torch.cuda.set_per_process_memory_fraction` 给分析进程设硬上限。第一次尝试时分析进程在 18.6GB 撞上自己的 cap 而 OOM，**训练进程毫发无损** —— 这个隔离机制是必要的。
- **数值精度**：SVD 与 Cholesky 全部在 fp64 下进行，最终转回 bf16 保存。Gram 矩阵以 fp32 保存，fp64 升级在每层内部按需进行，避免峰值内存。

### 非均匀 rank 分配

统一 rank=384 显然不是最优的 —— 各层的谱衰减差异很大。给定总参数预算 $B$，最优分配是一个可精确求解的问题：

$$\min_{\{r_i\}} \sum_i w_i E_i(r_i) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_i (m_i+n_i) r_i \le B$$

其中 $E_i(r) = \sum_{j>r} \sigma_{ij}^2$ 是 rank-$r$ 最优逼近的平方误差（Eckart–Young）。

**为什么贪心是精确最优的**：$E_i$ 关于 $r$ 是离散凸的（增量 $\sigma_{i,r+1}^2$ 单调递减），且问题可分离。对"可分离 + 离散凸"的预算分配，按边际收益贪心分配是**全局最优**（Fox 1966 的 marginal allocation 定理），不是启发式。

等价的拉格朗日形式给出一个**全局水位** $\lambda^*$：

$$r_i^* = \#\left\{ j : \frac{w_i \sigma_{ij}^2}{m_i+n_i} > \lambda^* \right\}$$

即"保留所有单位参数收益超过全局阈值的方向"。这句话点出了均匀 rank 的错误：均匀 rank 用的是"每个矩阵数到第 384 个"的**个数阈值**，最优解用的是**全局价值阈值**。

**权重 $w_i$ 的选择是一个真实的决策，不是细节：**

- $w_i = 1$ → 最小化**绝对**误差
- $w_i = 1/\|W_iS_i\|_F^2$ → 最小化**相对**误差（尺度无关，每层"平等"）

先看诊断数据（激活加权范数下）：

| layer | $\|WS\|_F$（尺度） | top-384 能量占比（可压缩性） |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3.35 | 0.5746 |
| 4 | 47.68 | 0.7119 |
| 12 | 19.43 | 0.6065 |
| 20 | 26.72 | 0.6197 |
| 28 | 67.06 | 0.5945 |
| 35 | **238.10** | 0.7346 |

**范数跨度 71 倍，而可压缩性几乎是平的（0.57–0.73）。** 所以两个目标给出的分配几乎相反：

| 目标 | layer 0 | layer 35 | o_proj | gate_proj |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| absolute | 32 | **1687** | 190.8 | 456.7 |
| relative | 673 | 471 | **953.4** | 152.7 |

absolute 目标把 rank 压倒性地投给深层（跨度 50 倍）；relative 目标下深度维度基本变平（437–673），改由投影类型主导，且顺序整个翻转。

**实测结果（全部 diag α=1.0，参数量均为 2.2925B）：**

| 分配 | 初始 val loss | 代理指标 abs err | 代理指标 rel err |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform 384 | 8.91 | 0.3384 | 0.6031 |
| **absolute** | **8.50** | **0.2474** | 0.7131 |
| relative | 9.43 | 0.3500 | 0.5601 |

**分析：**

1. **absolute 目标胜出，relative 甚至输给了 uniform。** 这否定了一个看似合理的先验推理：我们最初认为"深层范数大只是残差流范数随深度增长的量纲 artifact，且 LayerNorm 会洗掉范数信息，所以按范数分配 rank 缺乏机制上的理由"。实测表明这个推理是错的 —— 范数携带真实信号，深层输出误差在绝对尺度上确实更影响 loss。

2. **代理指标的可信度被区分开了**：abs err 与真实 val loss 排序完全一致（0.2474 < 0.3384 < 0.3500 ↔ 8.50 < 8.91 < 9.43），而 rel err 完全反向。后续做 rank 搜索时 abs err 可用作廉价代理，rel err 应丢弃。

3. **增益在快速递减**：换目标函数（plain→ASVD）值 −7.82；调 $\alpha$ 值 −1.92；换 rank 分配只值 −0.41。**rank 分配的杠杆比目标函数小一个数量级**，说明继续精雕初始化的回报正在耗尽。

### 初始化改进汇总

| 配置 | 初始 val loss |
|---|---|
| plain SVD, uniform（本次主训练所用） | 18.65 |
| diag-ASVD α=0.5, uniform | 10.83 |
| diag-ASVD α=1.0, uniform | 8.91 |
| whiten-ASVD, uniform | 8.64 |
| **diag-ASVD α=1.0 + absolute 分配** | **8.50** |

同参数量、零训练，从 18.65 降到 8.50（**−54.4%**）。

但这引出了唯一真正重要的问题：**这个改进能穿透训练吗？** 下一节用一个对照实验回答它。

### 决定性实验：初始化优势能否穿透训练？

前面所有初始化的比较都是**零训练**下的。这类数字很容易让人高估其价值 —— 如果几百步之后两个模型收敛到同一条曲线，那么把零训练 loss 从 18.65 优化到 8.50 就毫无意义。

于是做了一个双臂对照实验，**唯一差异是初始化**：

| 臂 | 初始化 | 初始 val loss |
|---|---|---|
| A（对照） | plain SVD，均匀 rank=384 | 18.61 |
| B（实验） | diag-ASVD α=1.0 + absolute 分配 | 8.49 |

两臂参数量均为 2.29B，同 seed 42、同数据顺序、global batch 2.097M tokens/step，各占一张 GPU 并行训练，每 50 步 eval。

**结果：**

| step | tokens | A plain | B asvd | B−A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 18.613 | 8.489 | **−10.124** |
| 50 | 0.105B | 8.098 | 7.718 | −0.381 |
| 100 | 0.210B | 7.713 | 7.188 | −0.525 |
| 150 | 0.315B | 7.430 | 6.667 | **−0.763**（峰值） |
| 200 | 0.419B | 6.918 | 6.284 | −0.634 |
| 250 | 0.524B | 6.510 | 6.075 | −0.435 |
| 300 | 0.629B | 6.146 | 5.871 | −0.274 |
| 350 | 0.734B | 5.731 | 5.613 | −0.118 |
| 400 | 0.839B | 5.421 | 5.246 | −0.176 |
| 450 | 0.944B | 5.163 | 5.037 | −0.126 |
| 500 | 1.048B | 4.847 | 4.809 | −0.038 |
| **550** | **1.153B** | **4.510** | **4.613** | **+0.102** |

**结论：初始化优势不能穿透训练。10.124 → 0.038 → +0.102 —— 不只是衰减 99.6%，而是穿过零点变成了劣势。** step 350 之后差值已不再单调（0.118 → 0.176 → 0.126 → 0.038 → −0.102），进入 eval 噪声量级并在其中震荡。B 臂的对照在 step 550 停止（A 臂结论已明确，停掉以释放 GPU），此后 B 臂单独续跑至 step 1100（2.31B tokens，val loss 3.617）。

曲线形态出人意料，分三个阶段：

1. **急速塌缩（0→50 步）**：差距蒸发 96%。A 臂从 18.61 一步冲到 8.10，仅 50 步（0.105B tokens）就走完了 ASVD 靠更好目标函数免费获得的全部距离，且反超了 B 的起点。这一段是"自由落体"—— A 在修补最粗糙的错误，与初始化质量无关。
2. **短暂反弹（50→150 步）**：差距从 0.381 回升到 0.763。当时我们怀疑这是 B 的结构性优势开始显现。
3. **持续收敛（150→500 步）**：0.763 → 0.038，几何衰减，每 50 步约衰减 35%。基于 step 300 的外推预测 step 500 为 0.05，实测 0.038，外推成立。

第 2 阶段的反弹现在看更可能只是两臂脱离各自初始瞬态的时间不同步，而非真实的持久优势 —— 因为它随后完整地消退了。

**这个结果重新定位了前面所有初始化工作的价值。** 需要区分两个场景：

- **免训练压缩**（推理部署、模型瘦身）：目标函数的选择极其重要。18.65 vs 8.50 是真实且决定性的差异，ASVD 那条线的工作完全有效。
- **继续预训练**：初始化质量几乎不重要。1B tokens 之后，一个"好"的分解和一个"差"的分解无法区分。

两个场景结论不冲突，但适用范围必须讲清楚。据此，两项原计划的后续工作被取消：

| 计划项 | 决定 | 理由 |
|---|---|---|
| whiten + absolute 合并（预期 8.3） | 放弃 | 为 0.2 的初始改进投入，300 步后剩不到 0.05 |
| Fisher 加权 rank 分配 | 放弃 | 同理 |

**真正的瓶颈不在起点，而在低秩容量的天花板**（下文 scaling law 中的 $E=2.372$）。

### 为什么权重拟合得好，hidden state 依然漂移？

上面的结论只对"继续预训练"成立。若目标是**免训练压缩**（压完直接用），那 8.50 仍然太差，值得追问：**权重在加权范数下拟合得很好，为什么 hidden state 还是偏离教师很远？**

我们逐层测了学生与教师的 hidden state 偏离（$\mathrm{rel}=\|h^S-h^T\|/\|h^T\|$，cos 为方向相似度）：

| layer | plain SVD rel | plain cos | ASVD rel | ASVD cos | $\|h_T\|$ | ASVD $\|h_S\|$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 31.64 | **−0.016** | 0.779 | 0.645 | 23.01 | 9.23 |
| 12 | 13.31 | −0.014 | 0.854 | 0.517 | 69.32 | 30.18 |
| 20 | 8.18 | −0.010 | 0.853 | 0.522 | 120.99 | 57.84 |
| 28 | 2.78 | −0.012 | 0.794 | 0.615 | 436.33 | 309.41 |
| 36 | 0.89 | 0.387 | **0.384** | **0.946** | 310.97 | 325.11 |

两个发现：

1. **plain SVD 不是"精度损失"，而是灾难性失效。** cos ≈ 0 意味着学生的表示与教师**正交**——36 层里有 32 层在处理与输入无关的信号，范数爆到 ~950 的常量平台。18.65 的 loss 不是"差一点"，是模型坏了。这也修正了一个可能的误解：ASVD 相对 plain SVD 的贡献不是"更准"，而是**让表示得以存活**。
2. **ASVD 的误差不沿深度累积**（layer 4→34 稳定在 0.73~0.88），说明残差连接和 LayerNorm 在起稳定作用。但中间层 cos 仅 ~0.5，而最后一层达 0.946 —— 表示在中途大幅偏离，却在输出端重新对齐。

#### 是哪个算子的问题？

我们用 teacher forcing 逐算子归因：喂给 block 教师的真实输入，**只分解一种算子**，其余保持满秩，测该 block 的输出误差。

| 算子 | L0 | L6 | L18 | L35 | L6 保留能量 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| q_proj | 0.165 | 0.023 | 0.022 | 0.021 | 0.833 |
| k_proj | 0.104 | **0.012** | **0.010** | 0.055 | 0.926 |
| v_proj | 0.250 | 0.069 | 0.025 | 0.070 | 0.759 |
| o_proj | 0.406 | 0.141 | 0.042 | 0.044 | 0.509 |
| **gate_proj** | 0.407 | **0.522** | 0.053 | 0.159 | **0.990** |
| up_proj | 0.380 | 0.242 | 0.067 | 0.223 | 0.340 |
| down_proj | **0.520** | 0.102 | 0.072 | 0.087 | 0.347 |

**结论：MLP 是主犯，attention 基本无辜。**

- **softmax 放大假设被否定**：q/k 是全模型误差最小的算子（0.010~0.055）。softmax 对输入平移不变，且 attention 只需要相对大小正确，所以对权重扰动相当鲁棒。
- **没有数值溢出**：全部配置 inf/nan 计数为 0。但确实观测到 **massive activations**（L6 的 $\max|x|=10240$，L18 为 12544，而 hidden norm 仅 50~104）。bf16 动态范围 $3.4\times10^{38}$ 装得下，不是溢出问题。
- **最关键的一行是 gate_proj**：它保留了 **99.0%** 的谱能量，却造成**最大**的 block 误差 0.522；而 up_proj 只保留 34% 的能量，误差反而只有它的一半。

这直接回答了标题的问题：**Frobenius 意义上"拟合得好"与 hidden state 正确是两件事。** `gate_proj` 的输出要过 SiLU 再做逐元素乘法，而 $\mathrm{silu}(g)=g\cdot\sigma(g)$ 在 $g\approx 0$ 附近非单调 —— 门控值的**符号**极度敏感，丢掉 1% 的能量足以让大量 token 的门从"开"翻成"关"。这是**离散的、非线性的失效**，任何 Frobenius 范数都看不见它。

#### 两个失败的修补尝试

**尝试一：给 gate_proj 更多 rank。** 既然它是主犯，就买更多容量。但预算算术先否定了"保持满秩"：

| | 参数量 |
|---|---|
| gate_proj 满秩（36 层 × 4096 × 12288） | **1811.9M** |
| 整个主体预算 | 1047.5M |

一个类型满秩就是总预算的 1.7 倍。改为在等预算下提高它的 rank，从别处扣：

| gate_proj rank | 其余层缩放 | 初始 val loss |
|---|---|---|
| ~380（基线） | 1.00× | **8.50** |
| 768 | 0.764× | 8.96 |
| 1024 | 0.570× | 9.59 |

**单调变差。** 这否定的是"加 rank"这个解法，而非"gate_proj 有问题"这个观察 —— 它已经有 99% 的能量，再加 rank 只是把 99% 变成 99.5%，而误差来源不在剩下那 1% 的**大小**，而在它**落在哪里**。翻转的临界 token 仍然翻转，而其他层降 24% 的容量损失是实打实的。

**尝试二：标量补偿幅度收缩。** 截断只会移除能量，所以 $\|ABx\|\le\|Wx\|$ 是**有偏**的，上表也显示 ASVD 的 $\|h_S\|$ 只有教师的 ~42%。于是给每个算子乘 $1/\sqrt{\text{保留能量}}$：

| block | ALL | ALL+rescale |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.693 | **0.769** |
| 6 | 0.606 | **0.171** |
| 18 | 0.094 | **0.179** |
| 35 | 0.306 | **0.408** |

四层里三层变差，只有 L6 大幅改善。**正确的补偿不是标量** —— 它必须是逐通道的、且只在需要时施加。

#### 根本问题：我们一直在优化代理目标

上述失败有一个共同根源。我们进一步测了每个 block 的**隔离损害** $\delta_\ell$（喂教师真实输入，只换该 block 的权重），并与驱动 rank 分配的代理 $\|W S\|_F$ 对比：

| layer | $\delta_\ell$（真实损害） | $\|WS\|_F$（分配依据） | 分到的 rank |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.780 | 13.21（最小） | **32**（最少） |
| 6 | **0.866**（最大） | 257.47 | 中等 |
| 18 | **0.104**（最小） | 66.35 | 少 |
| 35 | 0.117 | **879.79**（最大） | **1687**（最多） |

| 相关系数 | 值 |
|---|---|
| Pearson($\delta$, $\|WS\|_F$) | **−0.018** |
| Pearson($\delta$, $\log\|WS\|_F$) | −0.104 |
| 秩相关 | +0.079 |

**三个都是零。** 水填充在数学上严格最优（Fox 1966 的论证没错），但**它最优化的目标与实际损害无关**。

原因是残差流：深层 $\|WS\|_F$ 大（879 vs 13，跨度 67 倍），但深层的残差流本身也大（$\|h\|$ 从 1.68 涨到 863）。同样的权重扰动叠加到大得多的残差上被稀释，LayerNorm 又归一化掉幅度误差。**代理度量的是绝对扰动，实际损害取决于扰动相对残差流的比例** —— 两者沿深度反向变化，几乎完全抵消。

这解释了为什么所有闭式方法都会撞墙：SVD、ASVD、水填充都是对**单个矩阵在某个加权范数下**的一次性最优投影，而我们要的是**整个 block 在真实前向下**的输出正确。后者跨越 softmax、SiLU、残差、LayerNorm，没有闭式解，且与前者不相关。

#### 误差究竟出在哪一层？单块归因

"每部分逼近都好、整体很差"这个矛盾，此前所有测量都没能回答，因为它们测的都是**局部**量。一个 block 的 0.15 相对误差，可能被下游 30 层放大，也可能被残差稀释 —— 局部误差不等于对最终 loss 的贡献。

于是做单块归因：**只分解第 $L$ 个 block，其余 35 层保持满秩**，测端到端 val loss。

| block | val loss | vs 教师（2.3328） |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **10.2616** | **+7.9287** |
| 35 | 2.5384 | +0.2056 |
| 9 | 2.4202 | +0.0873 |
| 6 | 2.4167 | +0.0839 |
| 1 | 2.3792 | +0.0463 |
| 其余 | ~2.40 | ~+0.07 |

**block 0 一层贡献 +7.93，第二名只有 +0.21 —— 相差 39 倍。** 只分解第一层，loss 就超过了整个模型全分解的 8.50。

回头看，此前每一项测量都在指向 block 0，我们却都当成"它比较难压"而没深究：

| 指标 | block 0 | 其他层 |
|---|---|---|
| 隔离损害 $\delta$ | 0.780（最高） | 0.09~0.22 |
| hidden state cos | 0.73（最低） | 0.95~0.98 |
| 范数比 $\|h_S\|/\|h_T\|$ | 0.556（收缩最严重） | 0.90~1.00 |
| 边际曲线 | 要到 rank 1536 才见效 | rank 384 早已饱和 |
| 逐层重建增益 | 1.23×（最难改进） | 最高 7.24× |
| **分到的 rank** | **32（全模型下限）** | 380~1687 |

**根源是 rank 分配把它饿死了。** `absolute` 水填充按 $\|WS\|_F$ 分配，而 block 0 紧接 embedding，残差流范数只有 1.68（深层是 863），$\|WS\|_F$ 因此是全模型最小的 13.21。算法据此判定它"不值得给 rank"：

| block 0 的 7 个矩阵 | 满秩 | rank=32 后 |
|---|---|---|
| q/k/v/o + gate/up/down 合计 | 192.9M | **2.4M（1.2%）** |

整个第一层被压到 1.2%。而全模型 **48%（122/252）的矩阵都趴在 rank=32 的地板上**（中位数 38.5，均值 357.3），高 rank 全部集中在最后 6 层 —— block 35 分到的预算是 block 0 的 **61 倍**。

#### 读出层敏感度：一个必要的对照

在归因之前必须排除一种可能：也许 loss 崩坏与任何 block 都无关，而是**读出层对 hidden state 本身就极度敏感**。于是给教师的最终 hidden state 注入**同等相对幅度的随机噪声**再读出：

| 注入噪声（相对幅度） | val loss |
|---|---|
| 0（教师） | 2.3328 |
| 0.05 | 2.3783 |
| 0.10 | 2.5092 |
| 0.20 | 3.0278 |
| **0.384**（学生实测漂移） | **5.1952** |
| 0.60 | 9.8685 |

**仅在最后一步注入 38% 的纯随机噪声，loss 就从 2.33 涨到 5.20。** 这说明 8.50 里有很大一部分不是"某个算子坏了"，而是 hidden state 只要偏离这个量级，读出必然崩 —— 即使偏离完全随机、不含任何系统性错误。

这个对照同时给"免训练可用"设了一个现实下限：要让 loss 进到 3~4，需要的 hidden state 保真度远高于 rank 384 能提供的。

#### 四次修补尝试，全部失败

有了 block 0 的归因，修补方向似乎显而易见。但等预算约束下，全部失败：

| 尝试 | 配置 | 初始 val loss |
|---|---|---|
| **基线** | ASVD α=1.0 + absolute 分配 | **8.50** |
| gate_proj 加 rank | 768（其余 ×0.764） | 8.96 |
| gate_proj 加 rank | 1024（其余 ×0.570） | 9.59 |
| 标量幅度补偿 | $1/\sqrt{\text{保留能量}}$ | 4 层中 3 层变差 |
| teacher-forcing 逐层重建 | 36 层，每层 600 步 Adam | 12.75 |
| 顺序逐层重建 | 同上，但输入用学生自己的激活 | 10.54 |
| **block 0 满秩** | 192.9M（其余 ×0.818） | **9.84** |
| **block 0,1,2 满秩** | 578.8M（其余 ×0.452） | **8.55** |

最后两行揭示了一个反直觉的结构：**给 3 层满秩（其余压到 0.452×）远好于只给 1 层满秩（0.818×）**，8.55 vs 9.84。这否定了"其余层降 18% 的代价超过收益"这个解释 —— V2 把其余层降了 55%，代价理应更大。

真正的机制是**前段层必须作为整体被保护**：只修 block 0 而 block 1、2 仍在 rank 32 附近，等于把瓶颈往后挪一层，误差在第 1、2 层重新注入，block 0 花掉的 192.9M 白费。前段是串联的。

而逐层重建的失败同样有明确原因。teacher-forcing 模式下每个 block 的误差都下降（平均 1.45×，block 6 和 16 甚至有 6.06× 和 7.24× 的大幅改进），端到端却是 12.75 —— 因为每个 block 都是用**教师的干净输入**优化的，推理时拿到的却是上游学生的带误差输出，这是经典的曝光偏差。改成顺序模式（用学生自己的激活作输入）改善到 10.54，但仍输给闭式解：顺序模式下误差沿深度单调累积（block 7 的 0.28 → block 28 的 0.52），而每层只有 1.05× 的边际改进能力 —— 每个 block 要在"修正上游误差"和"保住自己的变换精度"之间取舍，而它只有 rank 384 的容量。**这是跨层的容量分配问题，逐层贪心解不了。**

#### 与文献的对照：这个压缩率超出了已有方法的能力

我们独立撞上的几乎每个发现，文献里都有对应工作，且大多是 2025–2026 年的密集产出：

| 我们的发现 | 文献 |
|---|---|
| 目标函数应为 $\|WX-ABX\|$ | ASVD、SVD-LLM |
| 非均匀 rank 分配 | Duo-SVD、ARA、Zero Sum SVD、Swift-SVD、IO-SVD、LACE-SVD（至少 6 篇） |
| $\|WS\|_F$ 代理与真实损害不相关 | Zero Sum SVD 直指"对损失敏感度差异巨大的同尺寸矩阵却用同样的 rank"；LACE-SVD："rank 预算的分配没有显式考虑 layer-wise 损失敏感度" |
| 逐层重建的误差累积、贪心不够 | SAES-SVD 完整命中："现有方法独立压缩每层、最小化各自重建误差，忽略了重建误差会在网络中传播并累积" |
| massive activations（实测 $\max\|x\|=12544$） | Massive Activations in LLMs (2024)；《A Single Layer to Explain Them All》(2026) 发现它们在特定"Massive Emergence Layer"产生后经残差传播 —— 或许正解释了 block 0 的特殊性 |
| **第一层异常脆弱（39 倍）** | **未见明确论述**，这一点可能是独立发现 |

文献中方法论更先进的几点：**Dobi-SVD** 主张"SVD 的最优用法是**截断激活**，而非仅把激活当作优化距离"；**AIR** 用反向信号影响度（梯度）做敏感度，即我们提过又放弃的 Fisher 加权，报告 >18% 改进；**LACE-SVD / Zero Sum SVD** 直接用校准集 NLL 增量做分配依据。

**但最关键的一个对照是压缩率。** LACE-SVD 作为 SOTA，在 LLaMA-7B、**压缩率 0.6** 上是 WikiText-2 PPL **32.57**（Dobi-SVD 46.18）。PPL 32.57 约合 loss 3.48 —— 而那只压掉了 40%。

**我们压掉了 85%（6945.8M → 1047.5M 主体参数），拿到 loss 8.50。**

数据集不同不可直接比，但量级足以说明问题：**在 85% 这个压缩率下追求"免训练可用"，文献里没有任何方法做到过；SOTA 在 60% 压缩率上就已经是 PPL 32 了。** 8.50 不是方法差，而是任务设定本身远超当前所有方法的能力边界 —— 这恰恰是这条路线的价值所在：它标定了一个此前没人测过的极端压缩区间，并给出了该区间内的失败模式图谱（第一层坍塌、代理指标失效、逐层贪心的容量分配困境）。

据此，"免训练可用"这个目标在 rank 384 下不成立，**瓶颈是秩本身，而非分解方法**。若要继续，文献指出三条路径：用校准集 NLL 增量替代范数做分配（LACE-SVD 路线，我们已有单块归因的基础设施）、inter-layer error compensation（SAES-SVD，修逐层贪心的问题）、或把压缩率降到文献验证过的 60~70% 区间。

### 从函数逼近论重新审视：我们一直在解错误的问题

上面那一连串失败有一个共同的结构性错误，而它在逼近论的语言下是显然的：**我们要逼近的是复合映射 $F=f_L\circ\cdots\circ f_1$，却一直在逐个逼近它的因子。**

#### 证据：误差不可加，而且会相消

这不是抽象的顾虑，而是可以直接测量的。把 36 个单块归因的 $\Delta$ 相加：

| 量 | 值 |
|---|---|
| 36 个单块 $\Delta$ 之和 | 10.10 |
| 若误差可加，预测 loss | 2.33 + 10.10 = **12.43** |
| 实测全模型 loss | **8.50** |
| **亚线性因子** | **1.64×** |

更强的证据在累积 prefix 曲线里 —— **增量大量为负**：

| 分解范围 | val loss | 增量 |
|---|---|---|
| 0..0 | 10.26 | **+7.93** |
| 0..1 | 9.11 | **−1.15** |
| 0..3 | 10.37 | **−3.20** |
| 0..15 | 9.88 | **−3.07** |
| 0..19 | 9.74 | **−2.94** |
| 0..23 | 8.48 | −0.97 |
| 0..35 | 8.37 | −0.11 |

**多分解一层，loss 反而下降 3.20。** 在逐层逼近的框架里这是不可能的 —— 那个框架下每层只能增加误差。**后面的层在补偿前面层的误差。**（曲线在 block 23 之后基本平坦，8.48 → 8.37，说明后 12 层几乎不贡献误差，尽管它们分到了最多的 rank。）

#### 为什么逐层目标是错的层次

记 $\varepsilon_\ell=f_\ell-\tilde f_\ell$，对复合映射作 telescoping 展开：

$$F-\tilde F=\sum_{\ell}\big(\partial f_{>\ell}\big)\,\varepsilon_\ell\circ\tilde f_{<\ell}+O(\varepsilon^2)$$

逐层目标隐含假设各项**正交**（误差平方可加）。实测 1.64× 的亚线性说明

$$\big\langle(\partial f_{>\ell})\varepsilon_\ell,\ (\partial f_{>k})\varepsilon_k\big\rangle<0$$

即**误差向量之间存在显著负相关**。于是逐层最小化 $\|\varepsilon_\ell\|$ 不只是"代理不准"，而是解了一个错误的问题：

- 它在一个不可加的目标上强行做可加分解；
- 它**禁止了误差相消这个自由度** —— 实测这个自由度值 1.64×；
- 这解释了我们逐层重建为什么反而更差（12.75 / 10.54 vs 闭式 8.50）：优化确实让每层的 $\|\varepsilon_\ell\|$ 变小了，却**破坏了 ASVD 的解碰巧保留的相消结构**。

展开式里的两个因子也解释了此前所有反常现象：

- $\partial f_{>\ell}$（**下游放大因子**）：block 0 的误差要穿过 35 层，block 35 的则直达输出 —— 这就是 **+7.93 vs +0.21** 的来源。而 $\|WS\|_F$ 是纯前向量，完全不含这个因子，所以 Pearson $=-0.018$ 是**必然的**，不是巧合。一阶地看正确的权重应是 $w_\ell=\|\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial h_\ell\|^2$（后向量，即 Fisher / AIR 那条线）。
- $\circ\,\tilde f_{<\ell}$（**学生的推前测度**）：误差必须在 $\tilde\mu_{\ell-1}$ 上度量，而 teacher forcing 用的是 $\mu_{\ell-1}$ —— 这正是 12.75 的成因，也是改用顺序模式能改善到 10.54 的原因。

#### 顺带：在当前逼近类里我们已经触到下界

设 $\mathcal{M}_r=\{AB\}$。ASVD 求解的是 $\min_{\hat W\in\mathcal{M}_r}\|(W-\hat W)S\|_F$，白化后由 Eckart–Young **精确取到**

$$\inf_{\hat W\in\mathcal{M}_r}\|(W-\hat W)S\|_F^2=\sum_{k>r}\sigma_k^2(WS)$$

这是 Kolmogorov $r$-宽度，**与算法无关的下界**。而我们实测 top-384 的能量占比仅 0.57~0.73，即 $\sigma_k$ 衰减极慢 —— **这些矩阵本来就不低秩**。所以"加 rank / 换分配 / 逐层优化"全部失败并非调参不足：在这个逼近类和这个度量下，闭式解已在下界上。

$\mathcal{M}_r$ 更深的结构缺陷是**值域约束**：$\operatorname{range}(AB)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(A)$，维数 $r$。**每层输出被钉死在一个与输入无关的固定 $r$ 维子空间里。** 这解释了算子间的差异 —— attention 的 $q/k$ 只进入双线性型 $x^\top W_q^\top W_k y$，内在秩本受 head_dim=128 限制，低秩几乎无损（误差 0.010~0.055）；而 MLP 要往残差流写高维信息，值域约束直接咬住（0.10~0.52）。也解释了 $E=2.372>2.00$ 为何持久：值域约束是**流形性质**，梯度下降动不了它 —— 不像初始化，500 步就抹平。

（附带一个数据点：我们此前测过 hidden state 的内在维度，95% 方差需要 1730~2038 维，而非 4096。数据确实住在低维流形上，但我们的逼近是在整个 $\mathbb{R}^{4096}$ 上做**全局线性**逼近，这本身就是浪费。样条/局部化、Kronecker 和 $\sum_i A_i\otimes B_i$（同等参数下单项即满秩，且由 Van Loan–Pitsianis 重排仍有闭式解）、以及乘性结构 $(A_1B_1x)\odot\sigma(A_2B_2x)$ 都是值得考虑的替代字典。但下面的实验说明，**字典是二阶问题，目标函数的层次才是一阶问题**。）

#### 直接优化复合映射：唯一有效的方法

既然层次错了，就把目标提到正确的层次 —— 对**全部 36 层联合**优化端到端的散度：

$$\min_{\{A_\ell,B_\ell\}}\ \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(F(x)\,\|\,\tilde F(x)\big)+\mathrm{CE}+\alpha\sum_\ell\frac{\|h^S_\ell-h^T_\ell\|^2}{\|h^T_\ell\|^2}\Big]$$

前两项是复合映射层面的，第三项（沿用 LRC 的 activation clone 思想）给每一层提供锚点 —— 单一的输出端信号无法告诉第 $\ell$ 层该做什么。关键在于这是**联合优化**，所以误差相消结构是被允许甚至被鼓励的，而非像逐层重建那样被破坏。

从 ASVD 的解出发，单卡 200 步（约 3.3B 等效 tokens，55 分钟）：

| step | val loss |
|---|---|
| 0 | 8.4834 |
| 25 | 7.6570 |
| 50 | 7.1616 |
| 100 | 6.3960 |
| 150 | 6.1452 |
| **200** | **6.1208** |

放在一起看，**目标函数的层次是唯一真正起作用的变量**：

| 方法 | 目标函数的层次 | val loss |
|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | 单矩阵**权重**误差 | 18.65 |
| ASVD（该类中闭式最优） | 单矩阵**输出**误差 | 8.50 |
| 逐层重建（teacher forcing） | 单 **block** 输出误差 | 12.75 |
| 逐层重建（顺序） | 单 block，学生输入 | 10.54 |
| **端到端蒸馏（200 步）** | **复合映射** | **6.12** |

从"逐 block 逼近"上升到"复合映射逼近"值 **2.36 个 nat**；而所有停留在低层次的改进尝试（加 rank、标量补偿、block 0 满秩、逐层优化）**全部为负**。

**消融：层次比信号源重要一个数量级。** 三项损失各去掉一项，同样 200 步：

| 配置 | KL | CE | act clone | val loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 完整 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | **6.121** |
| 去 act clone | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0 | 6.252（−0.131） |
| 去 KL | 0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 6.150（−0.029） |

两项都有贡献但都很小。特别值得注意的是 **KL 只值 0.029** —— 训练中 KL 项数值降得最多（6.32 → 4.13），很容易据此误判它是主力，但数值下降大不等于对 val loss 有贡献。CE + activation clone 单独就能到 6.150。

反过来，activation clone 值 0.131，尽管它自身的 MSE 几乎不降（0.790 → 0.758）—— 这两件事不矛盾：它是约束优化路径的正则项，并不需要自己被最小化。

**于是真正的结论是**：2.36 nat 的改进几乎**全部来自"36 层联合优化"这件事本身**，而非任何特定的教师信号。三种配置的差异都在 0.13 以内，而它们与逐层重建（10.54）相差 4.4。**把目标从"逐 block"提到"复合映射"值 4.4 nat；在正确层次上换教师信号只值 0.1。** 这正是层次错误作为一阶问题、信号选择作为二阶问题的量化体现。

（一个实用推论：KL 需要教师的完整 logits，分块计算约占 30% 的步时，而它只值 0.029 —— 长跑去掉 KL 更划算。）

**延长到 1000 步：趋平确实是假象。** 用消融选出的配置（去掉 KL，CE + activation clone），单卡 4.4 小时：

| step | val loss | Δ |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 8.4834 | — |
| 100 | 6.6354 | −1.848 |
| 200 | 5.7664 | −0.869 |
| 400 | 4.9599 | −0.327 |
| 600 | 4.6294 | −0.116 |
| 800 | 4.5379 | −0.028 |
| 900 | 4.5343 | −0.004 |
| **1000** | **4.5341** | **−0.0002** |

200 步时的"趋平"（6.145 → 6.121）确实只是 OneCycleLR 衰减到底的假象 —— 给足步数后继续降到 **4.5341**。这一次是真收敛：最后 100 步仅降 0.0002。

**4.53 跨过了两个有意义的门槛：**

1. **低于噪声对照的 5.20** —— 即"给教师最后一层注入 38% 随机噪声"的水平。学生 hidden state 的保真度现在优于那个基准，说明它不再处于"表示已崩坏"的区间。
2. **接近从零训练 1.7B 在 step 500 的水平**（4.6368 @ 1.05B tokens），而蒸馏只用了 **65.5M tokens**（1000 步 × 8 accum × 8192），即 **1/16 的数据**。

于是完整的对照表变成：

| 方法 | 目标函数的层次 | tokens | val loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | 单矩阵**权重**误差 | 0 | 18.65 |
| ASVD（该类中闭式最优） | 单矩阵**输出**误差 | 0 | 8.50 |
| 逐层重建（teacher forcing） | 单 **block** 输出误差 | 0.05B | 12.75 |
| 逐层重建（顺序） | 单 block，学生输入 | 0.05B | 10.54 |
| 端到端蒸馏 200 步 | **复合映射** | 13M | 6.12 |
| **端到端蒸馏 1000 步** | **复合映射** | **65.5M** | **4.53** |

必须标注的一点：这个对比不能简单读成"蒸馏比预训练高效 16 倍"。蒸馏的 65.5M tokens 是**在教师监督下**的，其上限受教师约束（教师 val loss 2.33）；而自监督预训练没有这个上限。两者是不同性质的过程，只在"达到某个 loss 需要多少数据"这一个维度上可比。

4.53 与教师的 2.33 仍有 2.2 的差距。这个差距是低秩容量的天花板，还是 1000 步 / 65.5M tokens 的预算限制，尚未区分 —— 曲线虽已完全平坦，但那是在这个 LR schedule 下的平坦。

严格说这已不再是"免训练"，而是**极低成本的恢复训练**（55 分钟单卡）—— 而这恰好是文献中所有成功方法的共同形态：Sheared LLaMA 用 3% 算力、E³-Pruner 用 0.5B tokens、LRC 用 20B tokens。"压完直接用"在 85% 压缩率下不成立；但"压完 + 极少量恢复"是可行的，而这个恢复的正确目标必须是复合映射，不是权重矩阵。

#### 与 LRC 的对照：教师该不该被抛弃？

LRC（*A Token is Worth over 1,000 Tokens*，NeurIPS 2025）与本节的做法高度相关，但有一个根本的设计分歧。它的参数化是

$$W^S_{m,i}=W^T_{m,i}\,W^P_{m,i},\qquad m\in\{q,k,v,o,\text{up},\text{gate},\text{down}\}$$

**学生权重由冻结的教师权重乘一个可训练投影矩阵生成**；唯一的可训练参数是投影矩阵加 RMSNorm（后者占比 <1%），学生权重本身从不直接训练。它还有一个漂亮的 alignment-free 技巧：教师与学生 hidden 维度不同，常规特征蒸馏需要额外对齐矩阵，而 LRC 直接**复用生成权重的那个投影矩阵**去投影教师激活（因 transformer 模块输出本就是其输出投影权重的线性组合），零额外参数。它用 20B tokens 让 LRC-1.7B 达到 64.98，超过用 36T tokens 训练的 Qwen3-1.7B 的 63.17。

这恰好点破了 LoRD 前提中的一个选择。我们的设定是"SVD 之后**彻底抛弃**原权重"，A、B 完全自由训练；LRC 则**永久保留教师权重作为生成因子**。这可能正是我们初始化优势归零的结构性原因：自由训练的 A、B 可以漂到任意远处，教师信息没有任何锚点；而 LRC 的学生**结构上不可能漂移** —— 梯度只能调整投影方式，不能丢弃教师结构。换言之，那不是"更好的初始化"，而是**把教师变成了永久约束**。

（论文另有一个与我们相关的数据点：硬剪掉 Llama-7B 的 50% 会让性能从 63.25 崩到 48.98，佐证"硬截断丢失信息"是这类方法的核心痛点 —— 我们 block 0 被压到 1.2% 后的坍塌正是其极端版本。）

拟合 $L(D) = A \cdot D^{-\alpha} + E$（$D$ 为 B tokens）：

$$L(D) = 2.438 \cdot D^{-1.323} + 2.372 \qquad (R^2 = 0.99996)$$

### 与从零训练的对比：一个需要谨慎解读的结论

| | LoRD r384 | 从零训 1.7B |
|---|---|---|
| Scaling law | $2.438 D^{-1.323} + 2.372$ | $3.611 D^{-0.658} + 2.00$ |
| 指数 $\alpha$ | **1.323** | 0.658 |
| 不可约项 $E$ | **2.372** | **2.00** |
| 实测最好 val loss | 2.5450 @ 7.34B | 2.576 @ 25B |

**数据效率**（达到相同 loss 所需 tokens 之比）：

| LoRD step | LoRD tokens | val loss | 从零训需要 | 效率倍数 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 500 | 1.05B | 4.6368 | 1.61B | 1.54x |
| 1000 | 2.10B | 3.2874 | 4.79B | 2.29x |
| 1500 | 3.15B | 2.9045 | 8.20B | 2.61x |
| 2000 | 4.19B | 2.7404 | 11.11B | **2.65x** |
| 2500 | 5.24B | 2.6435 | 13.75B | 2.62x |
| 3000 | 6.29B | 2.5864 | 15.84B | 2.52x |
| 3500 | 7.34B | 2.5450 | 17.70B | 2.41x |

**必须诚实指出两点：**

1. **效率倍数已经在回落**：从 step 2000 的峰值 2.65x 降到 step 3500 的 2.41x。LoRD 的优势主要在早期，且正在被消耗。

2. **LoRD 的不可约项更高（2.372 vs 2.00）**，配合更大的指数（1.323 vs 0.658），意味着 **LoRD 收敛极快但会撞上一个更高的天花板**。两条曲线会交叉：按拟合外推，从零训在约 47B tokens 附近达到 2.377，之后将反超 LoRD。

对这个现象有两种解释，我们目前无法区分：

- **解释 A（真实的容量瓶颈）**：rank=384 的低秩结构从根本上限制了模型的表达能力。$AB$ 的秩上界是 384，而稠密矩阵可以达到 4096，某些必须的高秩变换永远学不出来。这与我们此前的 rank sweep 一致 —— 即使 rank=4096（参数量超过原模型）零训练 loss 仍有 2.11，说明逐矩阵分解本身有信息损失。
- **解释B（外推不可靠）**：7 个数据点全部落在 7.3B tokens 以内，用它们外推到 25B–100B 跨越了一个多数量级。$E$ 项在这种情况下常被高估 —— 实际曲线可能在后期偏离幂律形式。

**要区分这两种解释，必须把训练跑到 12000 步以上实测**。我们的拟合预测 step 12000 会是 2.406；如果实测显著低于此值，则解释 B 成立。

### 训练速度：一个未预期的发现

本次训练耗时远超预期（12000 步需 22.7 天，而从零训 1.7B 的同样步数约一周）。排查发现两个原因：

**原因一：`lord_pretrain.py` 漏了 FA2 和 Liger Kernel。** 从零训脚本显式传了 `attn_implementation="flash_attention_2"` 并调用 `apply_liger_kernel_to_qwen3()`，LoRD 脚本两者都没有，transformers 默认回落到 SDPA。

这不影响已有的 loss 对比结论（SDPA 与 FA2 数学等价，Liger 也是数值等价的融合 kernel），但确实影响速度。

**原因二（主因）：架构本身。** 我们实测了各配置的 fwd+bwd 单步耗时（4 层、seq 8192、rank 384）：

| 配置 | ms/step | 相对 SDPA |
|---|---|---|
| SDPA, plain（当前） | 523.5 | 1.000x |
| FA2, plain | 567.5 | 0.922x |
| FA2 + Liger | 823.9 | 0.635x |

**FA2 在这个配置下反而更慢，Liger 更慢。** （该 benchmark 与主训练共卡，存在干扰，数值不可作为绝对参考；但至少说明 FA2 缺失不是 22.7 天的主因。）

真正的原因是：**低秩分解省参数量和显存，不省计算深度。** LoRD 走的是 Qwen3-8B 的架构（36 层 × hidden 4096），而从零训的 1.7B 是 28 层 × hidden 2048。虽然 $x \to xB^T \to (xB^T)A^T$ 的总 FLOPs 少于稠密 $xW^T$，但计算图的深度和宽度都由 8B 架构决定。这是 LoRD 方法的一个固有代价，值得在设计时纳入考量：**参数量的压缩比并不等于训练速度的提升比。**

### 待验证的开放问题

1. ~~**初始化改进能否穿透训练？**~~ **已回答：不能。** 见上文双臂对照实验，10.124 → 0.038（500 步，1.05B tokens）。这也解释了为什么早先的 SVD-vs-random 对照看起来相反 —— 那是 11.68 vs 18.61 的巨大差距在 2000 步后仍留 3.41，而 8.49 vs 18.61 在 500 步内就归零。差异不在"初始化是否重要"，而在初始化差距的量级：随机初始化破坏的是模型的全部结构，而两种 SVD 变体的差异只是同一结构下的精度差别。
2. **不可约项 2.372 是真实的低秩瓶颈还是外推假象？** 需跑到 12000 步以上实测。**这是当前最有价值的开放问题** —— 既然初始化已被证明无关，LoRD 的成败完全取决于这个天花板。
3. **rank=384 是不是最优秩？** 已知 rank=1024 的初始 loss 更低但参数更多，rank=4096 仍给出 2.11。如果 2.372 的天花板由秩决定，那么在固定参数预算下寻找最优秩（更少层数换更高秩？）比继续优化初始化有意义得多。
4. **架构继承的代价**：LoRD 沿用 8B 的 36 层 × 4096，主体参数比 1.7B 少 26% 却更慢。若把省下的参数用于减少层数或提高秩，wall-clock 效率会如何变化？

### 知识存储与提取的理论基础

**Allen-Zhu & Li, "Physics of Language Models" 系列 (ICML/ICLR 2024-2025)** 是与本工作最直接相关的理论框架：
- **Part 3.1 (Knowledge Storage and Extraction)**：使用合成传记数据证明了**没有足够数据增强时，模型能记住知识但无法提取**。5 种多样化表述将准确率从 9.7% 提升到 96.6%。我们的 permutation 方法直接实现了他们的建议，并在真实 1.7B 模型和真实知识上验证了这一结论。
- **Part 3.3 (Knowledge Capacity Scaling Laws)**：量化了知识存储容量约 2 bits/parameter。我们 1.7B 模型的理论容量远超 14K facts 的需求，证实瓶颈在于训练效率和可提取性，而非存储容量。

### Reversal Curse 与排列训练

**Berglund et al. (2023), "The Reversal Curse"** 揭示了训练 "A is B" 的模型无法推断 "B is A"（GPT-4: 正向 79% vs 反向 33%）。我们的 permutation 方法本质上是对 reversal curse 的缓解——通过在不同上下文和顺序中呈现同一知识，强迫模型建立双向关联。

**Guo et al. (2024), "Semantic-aware Permutation Training (SPT)"** 将句子切分为语义单元后做排列，使反向问题表现接近正向。他们 permute 句内语义单元，我们 permute 样本内的知识事实——目标不同但方法论一致。

### 知识注入效率

**Lu et al. (2024), "Scaling Laws for Fact Memorization"** 发现记住 Wikidata 全部事实需要 1000B 参数训 100 epochs，且与 epoch 呈负指数关系。我们的 permutation 方法以仅 41M tokens 达到 81.2% 存储率，远超其 scaling law 预测——关键差异在于我们优化了数据增强策略。

**Muennighoff et al. (2023)** 和 **Hernandez et al. (2022, Anthropic)** 分别证明了简单重复数据的 ceiling（~4 epochs 有效）以及过度重复对模型能力的损害。我们的 permutation 方法通过创造 genuinely diverse 的训练实例绕过了这一限制。

### 低秩压缩与激活感知分解

**Hsu et al. (2022), "FWSVD"** 首次指出逐矩阵 SVD 的目标错位，用 Fisher 信息给权重加权。**Yuan et al. (2023), "ASVD"** 提出用激活幅值做对角缩放，是我们 diag 模式的来源。**Wang et al. (2024), "SVD-LLM"** 用 Cholesky 白化把截断变成输出误差意义下的严格最优解，是我们 whiten 模式的来源。

这些工作的共同目标是**推理时压缩** —— 分解后直接使用，不再训练，因此重点在于零样本保真度。LoRD 的场景不同：我们把分解结果当作**预训练的初始化**，后续有大量梯度更新来修复误差。这带来一个尚未被研究的问题：**对零样本保真度最优的分解，是否也是对后续训练最优的初始化？** 二者未必一致 —— 例如白化可能引入病态的条件数，反而拖慢收敛。

**Xia et al. (2024), "Sheared LLaMA"** 用结构化剪枝 + 继续预训练把 LLaMA2-7B 压到 1.3B/2.7B，与 LoRD 目标相同但手段不同（剪掉整个维度 vs 低秩分解）。他们证明了"压缩大模型 + 继续预训练"比从零训练更高效，这与我们观察到的约 4 倍数据效率提升一致。

### 本工作的独特贡献

| 贡献 | 之前的空白 |
|------|-----------|
| 在真实 1.7B 模型 + PopQA 上验证 permutation 效果 | Allen-Zhu 只用合成数据和小模型 |
| 量化 storage-retrieval gap (81.2% vs 43.6%) | 之前仅有定性分析 |
| 证明 base model 通用能力是检索效率的决定因素 (43.6% vs 85.8%) | 无先前系统性对比 |
| 41M tokens 定向注入 > 万亿 tokens 通用训练（在特定知识上） | 无直接对比 |
| LoRD：彻底丢弃原权重，从 $AB$ 继续预训练 | ASVD/SVD-LLM 面向推理压缩，而非预训练初始化 |
| diag-ASVD 最优指数为 $\alpha=1.0$ 而非论文的 0.5 | 之前的调参目标是压缩质量，而非可训练性 |

## 总结

1. 1.7B 模型在 ~98B tokens 训练后 val loss 约 2.58，符合 power law 预测
2. Scaling law 可以从早期数据点（5B tokens 内）准确外推后续趋势
3. 模型首先学会确定性模式（子词续写、标点），然后学习语义和知识
4. 在 unique data + 1 epoch 条件下不存在过拟合，val loss 单调下降
5. 文档边界是模型预测的最大困难点，反映了上下文依赖的本质
6. 知识记忆与训练数据中的曝光频率强相关：高频知识（>10 万次）被可靠记住，长尾知识（<100 次）几乎无法习得
7. 用低秩分解压缩 Qwen3-8B 作为预训练起点（LoRD），在 7.34B tokens 时达到 val loss 2.545，超过从零训练在 25B tokens 的终点 2.576；峰值数据效率约 2.65x（step 2000），但已回落到 2.41x
8. LoRD 的 scaling law 为 $L(D)=2.438D^{-1.323}+2.372$，指数远大于从零训的 0.658 但不可约项更高（2.372 vs 2.00）——**收敛更快但天花板更高**，两条曲线预计在约 47B tokens 处交叉。这是低秩容量瓶颈还是外推假象尚未确定
9. 低秩分解的目标函数至关重要：把 $\|W-AB\|$ 换成 $\|WX-ABX\|$，同参数量、零训练下 loss 从 18.65 降到 8.50（−54%）。目标函数的杠杆比 rank 分配大一个数量级
10. **但初始化质量穿不透训练**：双臂对照下 18.61 vs 8.49 的差距在 500 步（1.05B tokens）内衰减 99.6% 至 0.038。**免训练压缩**场景下目标函数极其重要，**继续预训练**场景下几乎无关 —— 这两个结论必须分开陈述
11. 参数量的压缩比不等于训练速度的提升比：LoRD 主体参数比 1.7B 少 26%，但因沿用 8B 架构（36层×4096），单步耗时反而更长
12. **逼近的"层次"是压缩问题里唯一真正重要的变量。** 逐矩阵、逐 block 的逼近目标在数学上就是错的层次——实测 36 个单块误差之和为 10.10（若可加则预测 loss 12.43），实际全模型仅 8.50，**亚线性因子 1.64×**，且累积曲线出现 **−3.20 的负增量**（多分解一层 loss 反而下降）：后面的层在补偿前面层的误差。逐层目标禁止了这个相消自由度，所以逐层优化反而更差（12.75 / 10.54 vs 闭式 8.50）
13. **改成对复合映射做 36 层联合优化，是唯一有效的方法**：8.50 → 4.53（1000 步、65.5M tokens、单卡 4.4 小时）。消融显示收益几乎全部来自"联合优化"本身而非特定教师信号（KL 只值 0.029，activation clone 值 0.131），即**层次是一阶问题（值 4.4 nat），信号源是二阶问题（值 0.1 nat）**
14. 在 85% 压缩率下"压完直接用"不成立（文献 SOTA 在 60% 压缩率上也才 PPL 32），但"压完 + 极少量恢复训练"可行——这也是 Sheared LLaMA（3% 算力）、E³-Pruner（0.5B tokens）、LRC（20B tokens）等成功方法的共同形态

## Scaling Law Fitting

### Formula

We fit a Chinchilla-style token scaling law (fixed model size N=1.7B, varying data D):

$$L(D) = a \cdot D^{-b} + c$$

where $D$ is training tokens in billions.

### Fitting Results

Using data from step 500 to step 2500 (1B-5.2B tokens), we obtain:

$$L(D) = 3.611 \cdot D^{-0.658} + 2.00$$

| Parameter | Value | Meaning |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| $a$ | 3.611 | Scaling coefficient |
| $b$ | 0.658 | Power-law exponent (close to Chinchilla's ~0.5) |
| $c$ | 2.00 | Irreducible loss (data entropy floor) |

| 10.5B | 2.81 | 2.77 | +0.04 |

## Knowledge Injection Experiment: Permutation-based Knowledge Injection

### Motivation

Our baseline model achieves only 10.6% on PopQA (random 500 questions), with 83% of test entities never appearing in training data. Can we rapidly inject this knowledge through targeted training?

### Method

**Core idea**: Randomly permute knowledge facts into training samples so the model cannot memorize "sample sequences" — it can only memorize the knowledge itself.

1. **Knowledge database**: Extract 14,221 unique knowledge triples (subject, property, object) from all 14,267 PopQA questions
2. **Diverse surface forms**: Each fact has 5 different phrasings (e.g., `"X was directed by Y"`, `"The director of X is Y"`, `"Y directed X"`)
3. **Random permutation**: Each 8192-token training sample randomly samples ~680 facts, randomly orders them, randomly selects a surface form for each
4. **Newline-separated**: Facts within a sample are separated by newlines, forcing the model to learn each fact independently
5. **5000 samples generated** (41M tokens), each fact exposed ~245 times on average
6. **Mixed with general data to prevent forgetting**: knowledge_ratio=0.9 (90% knowledge + 10% FineWeb-Edu)

### Training Configuration

| Parameter | Value |
|-----------|-------|
| Base checkpoint | step_12000 (25B tokens pre-trained) |
| Knowledge data | 5000 samples × 8192 tokens = 41M tokens |
| Mix ratio | 90% knowledge + 10% general |
| Learning rate | 5e-5 (cosine schedule) |
| Batch size | 4, grad_accum=4 |
| Total steps | 5000 |

### Training Curve

|------|-----------------|----------|-----------|

## LoRD: Compressing a Larger Model to Accelerate Pre-training

### Motivation

A fundamental waste in from-scratch pre-training is that the model must relearn all linguistic regularities from random initialization — regularities that already exist inside open-source large model weights. Can we "compress" Qwen3-8B's parameters down to 1.7B scale and use that as the pre-training starting point?

We call this method **LoRD (Low-Rank Distillation)**. The idea borrows from LoRA but inverts its goal: LoRA freezes the original matrix $W$ and adds a low-rank increment $AB$; LoRD instead uses $AB$ to **approximate** $W$, then **discards $W$ entirely**, keeping only $A, B$ for continued pre-training.

Each linear layer $W \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times n}$ is replaced by $A \in \mathbb{R}^{m \times r}$ and $B \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times n}$:

$$W x \approx A B x$$

At rank $r=384$, Qwen3-8B's 252 linear layers compress from 6945.8M to 1047.5M parameters (**15.1%**).

### Parameter Breakdown

The composition of the total parameter count matters, otherwise it is easy to misjudge whether the comparison is fair:

| Component | LoRD r384 | From-scratch 1.7B |
|---|---|---|
| Transformer body | **1047.5M** | **1409.3M** |
| embed_tokens | 622.3M | 311.2M (tied) |
| lm_head | 622.3M (untied) | — (tied) |
| norms | 0.3M | 0.3M |
| **Total** | **2.2925B** | **1.7206B** |

LoRD's total (2.29B) exceeds 1.7B, but the difference comes almost entirely from embeddings — Qwen3-8B has hidden=4096 (twice that of 1.7B) and does not tie word embeddings. **The Transformer body that actually does the computation is 26% smaller in LoRD than in the 1.7B model.** This favors LoRD: it achieves better results with a smaller body. Tying the embeddings would bring the total to 1.67B, smaller than the from-scratch model.

(To be honest: the embedding is inherited wholesale from Qwen3-8B with no compression, so that knowledge comes "for free".)

### Initialization Comparison: SVD vs Random

A small-scale validation first (rank=1024, single GPU, 2000 steps):

| Initialization | Step 0 | Step 2000 |
|----------------|--------|-----------|
| Random init | 18.61 | 6.39 |
| **SVD init** | 11.68 | **2.98** |

SVD initialization's advantage is not only at the starting point — **after 2000 steps the gap is still 3.41**. This shows low-rank decomposition genuinely preserves exploitable structure, not merely a better starting point.

### The Key Question: Is Per-Matrix SVD Optimal?

The naive approach does independent SVD per matrix, minimizing the **weight error** $\|W - AB\|_F$. But this is the wrong objective. What the model actually cares about is the **output error** $\|WX - ABX\|_F$, and the two coincide only when $\mathbb{E}[XX^T] \propto I$ — a condition LLM activations badly violate due to the well-known **outlier channels** (a few input dimensions with 10-100× the magnitude of others, the same phenomenon that motivated the SmoothQuant/AWQ line of quantization work).

We measured Qwen3-8B's activation statistics (8 batches, 65K tokens): in layer 0's `down_proj`, input-channel $\text{mean}|x_i|$ ranges from 0.0036 to 0.154, a **42.9× spread**. Plain SVD spreads the rank budget evenly across all directions, wasting substantial capacity on channels with 1/43 the magnitude.

This diagnosis also explains the most counterintuitive result in the rank sweep:

| rank | Params | Initial val loss |
|------|--------|-----------------|
| 4096 | 11.3B (**more than the original**) | 2.11 |
| 2048 | — | 9.71 |
| 1024 | — | 11.71 |
| 512 | — | 19.91 |
| 384 | 2.29B | 18.65 |

At rank=4096 the factorized model has more parameters than the original 8B, yet loss is still 2.11 (original ≈1.6). The reason: rank=4096 is lossless for the $4096 \times 4096$ square matrices, so all error comes from the 12288-dimensional MLP matrices — precisely where outliers are worst.

### Activation-aware SVD (ASVD)

The fix is a change of variables. Introduce an invertible $S \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$:

$$WX = \underbrace{(WS)}_{\text{factorize this}} \underbrace{(S^{-1}X)}_{\text{whitened activations}}$$

Truncate the SVD of $WS$ to obtain

$$A = U_r \sqrt{\Sigma_r}, \qquad B = \sqrt{\Sigma_r} V_r^T S^{-1}$$

Two choices of $S$:

**Variant 1 — diagonal scaling** (original ASVD paper): $S = \text{diag}(s_i)$ with $s_i = (\text{mean}|x_i|)^{\alpha}$. Cheap, trivial $S^{-1}$, numerically stable.

**Variant 2 — Cholesky whitening** (SVD-LLM / Swift-SVD): $SS^T = \Sigma = \mathbb{E}[XX^T]$. Then $S^{-1}X$ has identity covariance, making SVD truncation **provably optimal** for the output error (Eckart–Young applied in whitened coordinates). The cost is an $n \times n$ Gram matrix and triangular solves.

### Results (rank=384, initial val loss with zero training)

| Method | $\alpha$ | Initial val loss | vs plain SVD |
|--------|---------|-----------------|-------------|
| plain SVD | — | 18.65 | — |
| diag-ASVD | 0.25 | 18.69 | +0.04 |
| diag-ASVD | 0.5 (paper default) | 10.83 | **−7.82** |
| diag-ASVD | 0.75 | 9.35 | **−9.30** |
| **diag-ASVD** | **1.0** | **8.91** | **−9.74** |
| diag-ASVD | 1.25 | 9.11 | −9.54 |
| diag-ASVD | 1.5 | 10.09 | −8.56 |
| diag-ASVD | 2.0 | 11.79 | −6.86 |
| **whiten-ASVD** | — | **8.64** | **−10.01** |

**Analysis:**

1. **Same parameter count, zero training, loss drops from 18.65 to 8.64 (−54%).** The only change is switching the objective from weight error to output error, at the cost of 8-32 forward batches of statistics. This shows plain SVD **optimizes the wrong objective** for this task.

2. **$\alpha$ exhibits a threshold effect, not a smooth tradeoff**: at $\alpha=0.25$ the loss is 18.69, essentially indistinguishable from no scaling at all (18.65); but $\alpha=0.5$ drops sharply to 10.83. Below the threshold the benefit is zero; above it the benefit is large.

3. **The optimum is $\alpha=1.0$, higher than the paper's default 0.5.** $\alpha=1.0$ corresponds to $S = \text{diag}(\text{mean}|x_i|)$ — exact linear weighting by activation magnitude, a cleaner form. Beyond 1.0 it degrades monotonically (over-compensation).

4. **whiten (8.64) slightly beats the best diag (8.91)**, consistent with theory (whiten is the exact optimum, diag is its diagonal approximation). But the gap is only 0.27 while the compute cost is far higher: whiten needs 29GB to hold the Gram matrices, 45 minutes of CPU statistics, and per-layer fp64 Cholesky plus triangular solves. **diag with $\alpha=1.0$ is the practical sweet spot.**

5. **ASVD deliberately sacrifices weight-fitting accuracy.** In a synthetic experiment (256×256 compressed to rank 64, with 8 injected 30× outlier channels):

| Method | Output error | Weight error |
|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | 0.6167 | **0.6123** |
| diag-ASVD | 0.1189 | 0.6242 |
| whiten-ASVD | **0.1152** | 0.6339 |

ASVD's weight error is **larger** — it actively trades weight-fitting precision for output precision. That is exactly what it should do.

### Implementation Notes

- **Deduplicating activation statistics**: within a Qwen3 block, q/k/v_proj share one input (input_layernorm output) and gate/up_proj share another (post_attention_layernorm output). Accumulating statistics only for one representative per group cuts whiten-mode memory from 58GB to 29GB.
- **Statistics collection runs only the transformer body**: skipping lm_head avoids materializing an $8192 \times 151936$ logits tensor (~5GB in fp32).
- **Chunked eval loss**: applying lm_head in 1024-token chunks prevents OOM from cross_entropy upcasting the full logits to fp32.
- **GPU memory fencing**: since the main training run shares the same GPU, we cap the analysis process with `torch.cuda.set_per_process_memory_fraction`. On the first attempt the analysis process OOM'd against its own 18.6GB cap while **the training process was completely unaffected** — this isolation mechanism is necessary.
- **Numerical precision**: all SVD and Cholesky operations run in fp64, converted back to bf16 for saving. Gram matrices are stored in fp32 with the fp64 upcast done per-layer on demand, to avoid a memory spike.

### Non-Uniform Rank Allocation

A uniform rank=384 is clearly not optimal — spectral decay varies substantially across layers. Given a total parameter budget $B$, the optimal allocation is an exactly solvable problem:

$$\min_{\{r_i\}} \sum_i w_i E_i(r_i) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_i (m_i+n_i) r_i \le B$$

where $E_i(r) = \sum_{j>r} \sigma_{ij}^2$ is the squared error of the best rank-$r$ approximation (Eckart–Young).

**Why greedy is exact**: $E_i$ is discretely convex in $r$ (its decrements $\sigma_{i,r+1}^2$ are non-increasing) and the problem is separable. For separable + discretely convex budget allocation, greedy marginal allocation is **globally optimal** (Fox 1966's marginal allocation theorem) — not a heuristic.

The equivalent Lagrangian form yields a single global **water level** $\lambda^*$:

$$r_i^* = \#\left\{ j : \frac{w_i \sigma_{ij}^2}{m_i+n_i} > \lambda^* \right\}$$

i.e. "keep every direction whose benefit per parameter exceeds a global threshold." This pinpoints what uniform rank gets wrong: uniform rank uses a **count threshold** ("take the first 384 of every matrix") whereas the optimum uses a **global value threshold**. The two coincide only if all matrices have identically shaped spectra.

**The choice of $w_i$ is a real decision, not a detail:**

- $w_i = 1$ → minimize **absolute** error
- $w_i = 1/\|W_iS_i\|_F^2$ → minimize **relative** error (scale-invariant, every layer counts equally)

First the diagnostic (in the activation-weighted norm):

| layer | $\|WS\|_F$ (scale) | top-384 energy fraction (compressibility) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3.35 | 0.5746 |
| 4 | 47.68 | 0.7119 |
| 12 | 19.43 | 0.6065 |
| 20 | 26.72 | 0.6197 |
| 28 | 67.06 | 0.5945 |
| 35 | **238.10** | 0.7346 |

**The norm spans 71×, while compressibility is nearly flat (0.57–0.73).** Consequently the two objectives produce nearly opposite allocations:

| Objective | layer 0 | layer 35 | o_proj | gate_proj |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| absolute | 32 | **1687** | 190.8 | 456.7 |
| relative | 673 | 471 | **953.4** | 152.7 |

The absolute objective pours rank overwhelmingly into deep layers (50× spread); under the relative objective the depth dimension flattens out (437–673) and projection type takes over as the discriminator, with the ordering completely inverted.

**Measured results (all diag $\alpha=1.0$, all at 2.2925B parameters):**

| Allocation | Initial val loss | Proxy: abs err | Proxy: rel err |
|---|---|---|---|
| uniform 384 | 8.91 | 0.3384 | 0.6031 |
| **absolute** | **8.50** | **0.2474** | 0.7131 |
| relative | 9.43 | 0.3500 | 0.5601 |

**Analysis:**

1. **The absolute objective wins, and relative loses even to uniform.** This refutes a plausible-sounding prior we initially held: that the deep-layer bias was "a dimensional artifact of residual-stream norm growth with depth, and since LayerNorm washes out norm information, allocating rank by norm lacks a mechanistic justification." The measurement shows that reasoning was wrong — the norm carries real signal, and deep-layer output error genuinely matters more on an absolute scale.

2. **The two proxies are now distinguished in credibility**: abs err orders identically to true val loss (0.2474 < 0.3384 < 0.3500 ↔ 8.50 < 8.91 < 9.43), while rel err is exactly inverted. For future rank searches, abs err is a usable cheap proxy; rel err should be discarded.

3. **Returns are diminishing fast**: switching the objective (plain→ASVD) was worth −7.82; tuning $\alpha$ was worth −1.92; changing the rank allocation is worth only −0.41. **The rank-allocation lever is an order of magnitude smaller than the objective lever**, suggesting further polishing of the initialization is nearly exhausted.

### The Decisive Experiment: Does the Initialization Advantage Penetrate Training?

Every initialization comparison so far was measured with **zero training**. Such numbers invite overestimating their value — if the two models converge onto the same curve after a few hundred steps, then optimizing the zero-training loss from 18.65 to 8.50 means nothing.

**Conclusion: the initialization advantage does not penetrate training. 10.124 → 0.038 → +0.102 — not merely a 99.6% decay but a crossing of zero into a disadvantage.** Past step 350 the gap is no longer monotonic (0.118 → 0.176 → 0.126 → 0.038 → −0.102); it has entered eval-noise territory and now oscillates within it. The controlled comparison ended at step 550 (arm A's conclusion was settled, so it was stopped to free a GPU); arm B continued alone to step 1100 (2.31B tokens, val loss 3.617).

The shape of the curve was unexpected, with three phases:

1. **Rapid collapse (0→50 steps)**: 96% of the gap evaporates. Arm A goes from 18.61 to 8.10, covering in just 50 steps (0.105B tokens) the entire distance that ASVD obtained for free from a better objective — and overshooting B's starting point. This phase is free-fall: A is repairing the coarsest errors, which has nothing to do with initialization quality.
2. **Brief rebound (50→150 steps)**: the gap widens back from 0.381 to 0.763. At the time we suspected this was B's structural advantage emerging.
3. **Sustained convergence (150→500 steps)**: 0.763 → 0.038, a geometric decay of roughly 35% per 50 steps. Extrapolating from step 300 predicted 0.05 at step 500; the measured value was 0.038, so the extrapolation held.

The phase-2 rebound now looks more like the two arms exiting their respective initial transients out of sync, rather than a genuine lasting advantage — because it subsequently decayed away completely.

**This result repositions the value of all the preceding initialization work.** Two scenarios must be distinguished:

- **Training-free compression** (inference deployment, model slimming): the choice of objective matters enormously. 18.65 vs 8.50 is a real and decisive difference, and the entire ASVD line of work is fully valid.
- **Continued pre-training**: initialization quality barely matters. After 1B tokens, a "good" factorization and a "bad" one are indistinguishable.

The two conclusions do not conflict, but the scope of applicability has to be stated. On this basis, two planned follow-ups were cancelled:

| Planned item | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| whiten + absolute merge (expected 8.3) | Dropped | Investing for a 0.2 initial gain that leaves under 0.05 after 300 steps |
| Fisher-weighted rank allocation | Dropped | Same |

**The real bottleneck is not the starting point but the low-rank capacity ceiling** ($E=2.372$ in the scaling law below).

### Why Do Hidden States Drift When the Weights Fit Well?

The conclusion above holds only for continued pre-training. If the goal is **training-free compression** (use it as-is after compressing), 8.50 is still far too poor, and the question deserves an answer: **the weights fit well in the activation-weighted norm, so why do the hidden states still land so far from the teacher's?**

We measured the per-layer drift between student and teacher hidden states ($\mathrm{rel}=\|h^S-h^T\|/\|h^T\|$, with cos as directional similarity):

| layer | plain SVD rel | plain cos | ASVD rel | ASVD cos | $\|h_T\|$ | ASVD $\|h_S\|$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 31.64 | **−0.016** | 0.779 | 0.645 | 23.01 | 9.23 |
| 12 | 13.31 | −0.014 | 0.854 | 0.517 | 69.32 | 30.18 |
| 20 | 8.18 | −0.010 | 0.853 | 0.522 | 120.99 | 57.84 |
| 28 | 2.78 | −0.012 | 0.794 | 0.615 | 436.33 | 309.41 |
| 36 | 0.89 | 0.387 | **0.384** | **0.946** | 310.97 | 325.11 |

Two findings:

1. **plain SVD is not "a loss of precision" — it is catastrophic failure.** cos ≈ 0 means the student's representation is **orthogonal** to the teacher's: 32 of 36 layers are processing a signal unrelated to the input, with the norm blowing up to a near-constant plateau of ~950. A loss of 18.65 does not mean "slightly off"; the model is broken. This also corrects a likely misreading: ASVD's contribution over plain SVD is not "more accurate" but **keeping the representation alive at all**.
2. **ASVD's error does not accumulate with depth** (stable at 0.73~0.88 from layer 4 to 34), so the residual connections and LayerNorm are doing stabilizing work. But mid-layer cos is only ~0.5 while the final layer reaches 0.946 — the representation drifts badly in the middle and re-aligns at the output.

#### Which operator is at fault?

We attributed the damage per operator under teacher forcing: feed the block the teacher's true input, factorize **exactly one operator type**, keep the rest full-rank, and measure the block's output error.

| operator | L0 | L6 | L18 | L35 | L6 energy kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| q_proj | 0.165 | 0.023 | 0.022 | 0.021 | 0.833 |
| k_proj | 0.104 | **0.012** | **0.010** | 0.055 | 0.926 |
| v_proj | 0.250 | 0.069 | 0.025 | 0.070 | 0.759 |
| o_proj | 0.406 | 0.141 | 0.042 | 0.044 | 0.509 |
| **gate_proj** | 0.407 | **0.522** | 0.053 | 0.159 | **0.990** |
| up_proj | 0.380 | 0.242 | 0.067 | 0.223 | 0.340 |
| down_proj | **0.520** | 0.102 | 0.072 | 0.087 | 0.347 |

**The MLP is the culprit; attention is largely innocent.**

- **The softmax-amplification hypothesis is refuted**: q/k are the *lowest*-error operators in the whole model (0.010~0.055). Softmax is shift-invariant in its input and attention only needs the relative ordering to be right, making it quite robust to weight perturbation.
- **No numerical overflow**: inf/nan counts were 0 in every configuration. We did observe **massive activations** ($\max|x|=10240$ at L6, 12544 at L18, against a hidden norm of just 50~104), but bf16's dynamic range of $3.4\times10^{38}$ accommodates them.
- **The critical row is gate_proj**: it retains **99.0%** of its spectral energy yet causes the **largest** block error at 0.522, while up_proj retains only 34% of its energy for half the error.

That answers the question in the heading: **"fits well in the Frobenius sense" and "hidden states are right" are two different things.** `gate_proj`'s output passes through SiLU and then an elementwise product, and $\mathrm{silu}(g)=g\cdot\sigma(g)$ is non-monotonic near $g\approx 0$ — the **sign** of the gate value is extremely sensitive, and discarding 1% of the energy is enough to flip many tokens' gates from open to closed. This is a **discrete, non-linear** failure that no Frobenius norm can see.

#### Where does the error really come from? Single-block attribution

None of the measurements above could resolve the "each part approximates well, the whole is terrible" paradox, because they are all **local** quantities. A block's 0.15 relative error might be amplified by the 30 layers downstream or diluted away by the residual stream; local error is not a contribution to the final loss.

So: single-block attribution — **factorize only block $L$, keep the other 35 full-rank**, and measure end-to-end val loss.

| block | val loss | vs teacher (2.3328) |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | **10.2616** | **+7.9287** |
| 35 | 2.5384 | +0.2056 |
| 9 | 2.4202 | +0.0873 |
| 6 | 2.4167 | +0.0839 |
| 1 | 2.3792 | +0.0463 |
| rest | ~2.40 | ~+0.07 |

**Block 0 alone contributes +7.93; the runner-up contributes 0.21 — a factor of 39.** Factorizing just the first layer produces a worse loss than factorizing the entire model (8.50).

In hindsight every earlier measurement was pointing at block 0, and we kept reading it as "this one is harder to compress":

| metric | block 0 | other layers |
|---|---|---|
| isolated damage $\delta$ | 0.780 (highest) | 0.09~0.22 |
| hidden state cos | 0.73 (lowest) | 0.95~0.98 |
| norm ratio $\|h_S\|/\|h_T\|$ | 0.556 (worst shrinkage) | 0.90~1.00 |
| marginal curve | needs rank ~1536 to help | saturated well before 384 |
| layer-wise reconstruction gain | 1.23× (hardest to improve) | up to 7.24× |
| **allocated rank** | **32 (the global floor)** | 380~1687 |

**The root cause is that rank allocation starved it.** `absolute` water-filling allocates by $\|WS\|_F$, and block 0 sits right after the embedding where the residual stream norm is only 1.68 (versus 863 at depth), making its $\|WS\|_F$ the smallest in the model at 13.21. The algorithm concluded it "does not deserve rank":

| block 0's 7 matrices | full rank | at rank=32 |
|---|---|---|
| q/k/v/o + gate/up/down total | 192.9M | **2.4M (1.2%)** |

The entire first layer was compressed to 1.2%. Across the model, **48% (122/252) of the matrices sit on the rank=32 floor** (median 38.5, mean 357.3), with high ranks concentrated in the last 6 layers — block 35 received **61×** block 0's budget.

#### Readout sensitivity: a necessary control

Before trusting that attribution we had to rule out an alternative: perhaps the loss collapse has nothing to do with any block, and the **readout is simply hypersensitive to hidden-state error**. So we injected **random noise of matched relative magnitude** into the teacher's final hidden state and read out:

| injected noise (relative) | val loss |
|---|---|
| 0 (teacher) | 2.3328 |
| 0.05 | 2.3783 |
| 0.10 | 2.5092 |
| 0.20 | 3.0278 |
| **0.384** (student's measured drift) | **5.1952** |
| 0.60 | 9.8685 |

**Injecting 38% purely random noise at the very last step alone takes the loss from 2.33 to 5.20.** So a large part of the 8.50 is not "some operator broke" but the fact that once the hidden state deviates by this magnitude, the readout necessarily collapses — even when the deviation is entirely random and contains no systematic error.

This control also sets a realistic floor for "training-free usable": reaching a loss of 3~4 requires hidden-state fidelity far beyond what rank 384 can deliver.

#### Four repair attempts, all failed

With block 0 identified, the fix looked obvious. Under an equal parameter budget, every attempt failed:

| attempt | configuration | initial val loss |
|---|---|---|
| **baseline** | ASVD α=1.0 + absolute allocation | **8.50** |
| boost gate_proj | rank 768 (rest ×0.764) | 8.96 |
| boost gate_proj | rank 1024 (rest ×0.570) | 9.59 |
| scalar magnitude compensation | $1/\sqrt{\text{energy kept}}$ | 3 of 4 layers worse |
| teacher-forcing reconstruction | 36 blocks, 600 Adam steps each | 12.75 |
| sequential reconstruction | same, but student's own activations as input | 10.54 |
| **block 0 full-rank** | 192.9M (rest ×0.818) | **9.84** |
| **blocks 0,1,2 full-rank** | 578.8M (rest ×0.452) | **8.55** |

The last two rows reveal a counterintuitive structure: **giving 3 layers full rank (rest squeezed to 0.452×) is far better than giving 1 layer full rank (0.818×)** — 8.55 vs 9.84. That refutes the explanation "the cost of dropping other layers by 18% exceeds the gain," since V2 dropped them by 55% and should have cost more.

The actual mechanism is that **the early layers have to be protected as a group**: fixing block 0 while blocks 1 and 2 remain near rank 32 merely moves the bottleneck one layer down, the error is re-injected at layers 1–2, and block 0's 192.9M is wasted. The early stack is in series.

The reconstruction failures have an equally clear cause. Under teacher forcing every block's error fell (1.45× on average, with block 6 and 16 improving 6.06× and 7.24×), yet end-to-end was 12.75 — because each block was optimized on the **teacher's clean input** while at inference it receives the upstream student's noisy output. That is textbook exposure bias. Switching to sequential mode (using the student's own activations as input) improved it to 10.54, but it still loses to the closed-form solution: in sequential mode the error grows monotonically with depth (0.28 at block 7 → 0.52 at block 28) while each layer has only ~1.05× of marginal improvement available. Every block must trade off "correcting upstream error" against "preserving its own transform," and it only has rank 384 to spend. **This is a cross-layer capacity allocation problem, and layer-wise greedy cannot solve it.**

#### Against the literature: this compression ratio is beyond existing methods

Nearly every finding we arrived at independently has a counterpart in the literature, mostly from a dense burst of 2025–2026 work:

| our finding | literature |
|---|---|
| the objective should be $\|WX-ABX\|$ | ASVD, SVD-LLM |
| non-uniform rank allocation | Duo-SVD, ARA, Zero Sum SVD, Swift-SVD, IO-SVD, LACE-SVD (at least 6 papers) |
| the $\|WS\|_F$ proxy is uncorrelated with real damage | Zero Sum SVD notes prior work "often use homogeneous ranks for similarly sized matrices, despite large differences in loss sensitivity"; LACE-SVD: "rank budgets are often allocated without explicitly considering layer-wise loss sensitivity" |
| layer-wise reconstruction error accumulates; greedy is insufficient | SAES-SVD hits it exactly: "existing methods typically compress each layer independently by minimizing per-layer reconstruction error, overlooking a critical limitation: the reconstruction error propagates and accumulates through the network" |
| massive activations (measured $\max\|x\|=12544$) | *Massive Activations in LLMs* (2024); *A Single Layer to Explain Them All* (2026) finds they emerge in a specific "Massive Emergence Layer" and then propagate via residual connections — possibly the very reason block 0 is special |
| **the first layer is disproportionately fragile (39×)** | **no explicit treatment found**; this may be an independent finding |

Methodologically the literature is ahead of us in several places: **Dobi-SVD** argues "the optimal use of SVD lies in truncating activations, rather than merely using activations as an optimization distance"; **AIR** uses a backward-signal influence metric (i.e. the Fisher weighting we proposed and abandoned) and reports >18% improvement; **LACE-SVD / Zero Sum SVD** allocate directly by the calibration-set NLL increase.

**But the most important comparison is the compression ratio.** LACE-SVD, as SOTA, reports WikiText-2 PPL **32.57** on LLaMA-7B at a **0.6 compression ratio** (Dobi-SVD: 46.18). PPL 32.57 is roughly a loss of 3.48 — for removing only 40%.

**We removed 85% (6945.8M → 1047.5M body params) and got 8.50.**

The datasets differ so the numbers are not directly comparable, but the orders of magnitude make the point: **nobody in the literature has achieved "training-free usable" at an 85% compression ratio; SOTA is already at PPL 32 for 60%.** 8.50 does not indicate a weak method — the task setting itself lies well beyond the capability frontier of every current approach. That is precisely where this line of work has value: it charts an extreme compression regime nobody has measured, and produces a map of the failure modes within it (first-layer collapse, proxy-metric breakdown, and the cross-layer capacity dilemma that defeats layer-wise greedy).

On that basis, "training-free usable" is unattainable at rank 384, and **the bottleneck is the rank itself, not the factorization method**. To continue, the literature points to three routes: allocate by calibration NLL increase instead of norms (the LACE-SVD route, for which our single-block attribution machinery is already built), inter-layer error compensation (SAES-SVD, fixing the layer-wise greedy failure), or lower the compression ratio into the 60~70% band the literature has validated.

### Revisiting This From Approximation Theory: We Were Solving the Wrong Problem

That whole string of failures shares one structural error, and in the language of approximation theory it is obvious: **the object we need to approximate is the composite map $F=f_L\circ\cdots\circ f_1$, yet we kept approximating its factors one at a time.**

#### Evidence: the errors are not additive, and they cancel

This is not an abstract worry — it is directly measurable. Summing the 36 single-block $\Delta$s:

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| sum of the 36 single-block $\Delta$s | 10.10 |
| loss predicted if errors were additive | 2.33 + 10.10 = **12.43** |
| measured full-model loss | **8.50** |
| **sublinearity factor** | **1.64×** |

The cumulative-prefix curve makes the point even more sharply — **many increments are negative**:

| blocks factorized | val loss | increment |
|---|---|---|
| 0..0 | 10.26 | **+7.93** |
| 0..1 | 9.11 | **−1.15** |
| 0..3 | 10.37 | **−3.20** |
| 0..15 | 9.88 | **−3.07** |
| 0..19 | 9.74 | **−2.94** |
| 0..23 | 8.48 | −0.97 |
| 0..35 | 8.37 | −0.11 |

**Factorizing one more block sometimes drops the loss by 3.20.** That is impossible under a layer-wise view of approximation, where each additional layer can only add error. **The later layers are compensating for the errors of the earlier ones.** (The curve is essentially flat past block 23 — 8.48 → 8.37 — so the last 12 layers contribute almost no error despite receiving the largest rank allocations.)

#### Why per-layer objectives are the wrong level

Write $\varepsilon_\ell=f_\ell-\tilde f_\ell$ and telescope the composite:

$$F-\tilde F=\sum_{\ell}\big(\partial f_{>\ell}\big)\,\varepsilon_\ell\circ\tilde f_{<\ell}+O(\varepsilon^2)$$

A per-layer objective implicitly assumes these terms are **orthogonal** (errors adding in quadrature). The measured 1.64× sublinearity means

$$\big\langle(\partial f_{>\ell})\varepsilon_\ell,\ (\partial f_{>k})\varepsilon_k\big\rangle<0$$

i.e. **the error vectors are substantially negatively correlated**. Minimizing $\|\varepsilon_\ell\|$ per layer is therefore not just "an inaccurate proxy" — it solves the wrong problem:

- it forces an additive decomposition onto a non-additive objective;
- it **forbids the error-cancellation degree of freedom** — worth a measured 1.64×;
- which explains why our layer-wise reconstruction came out *worse* (12.75 / 10.54 vs the closed form's 8.50): it genuinely shrank every $\|\varepsilon_\ell\|$ while **destroying the cancellation structure that ASVD's solution happened to preserve**.

The two factors in the expansion also explain every anomaly we hit:

- $\partial f_{>\ell}$ (**the downstream amplification factor**): block 0's error must traverse 35 layers while block 35's goes straight to the output — that is the origin of **+7.93 vs +0.21**. And $\|WS\|_F$ is a purely forward quantity containing none of this factor, so Pearson $=-0.018$ was **inevitable**, not a coincidence. To first order the correct weight is $w_\ell=\|\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial h_\ell\|^2$ — a backward quantity, i.e. the Fisher / AIR line of work.
- $\circ\,\tilde f_{<\ell}$ (**the student's pushforward measure**): the error must be measured under $\tilde\mu_{\ell-1}$, whereas teacher forcing uses $\mu_{\ell-1}$ — precisely the cause of the 12.75, and the reason switching to sequential mode improved it to 10.54.

#### Incidentally: within this approximation class we already sit at the lower bound

Let $\mathcal{M}_r=\{AB\}$. ASVD solves $\min_{\hat W\in\mathcal{M}_r}\|(W-\hat W)S\|_F$, and after whitening Eckart–Young attains it **exactly**:

$$\inf_{\hat W\in\mathcal{M}_r}\|(W-\hat W)S\|_F^2=\sum_{k>r}\sigma_k^2(WS)$$

This is a Kolmogorov $r$-width — an **algorithm-independent lower bound**. Our measurements put only 0.57~0.73 of the energy in the top 384 directions, i.e. $\sigma_k$ decays very slowly: **these matrices simply are not low-rank**. So "add rank / change the allocation / optimize layer-wise" did not fail for want of tuning — in this class, under this metric, the closed form is already on the bound.

$\mathcal{M}_r$'s deeper structural defect is the **range constraint**: $\operatorname{range}(AB)\subseteq\operatorname{range}(A)$, of dimension $r$. **Every layer's output is pinned to a fixed $r$-dimensional subspace that does not depend on the input.** That explains the differences between operators — attention's $q/k$ only enter the bilinear form $x^\top W_q^\top W_k y$, whose intrinsic rank is already capped by head_dim=128, so low rank costs almost nothing (errors 0.010~0.055); the MLP must write high-dimensional information into the residual stream, where the range constraint bites (0.10~0.52). It also explains why $E=2.372>2.00$ persists: the range constraint is a **property of the manifold**, which gradient descent cannot alter — unlike an initialization, which 500 steps erase.

(One supporting data point: we had earlier measured the intrinsic dimensionality of the hidden states, and 95% of the variance needs 1730~2038 dimensions, not 4096. The data does live on a low-dimensional manifold — yet our approximation is **globally linear over all of $\mathbb{R}^{4096}$**, which is wasteful in itself. Splines/localization, Kronecker sums $\sum_i A_i\otimes B_i$ — where a single term is already full-rank at the same parameter cost, and the Van Loan–Pitsianis rearrangement keeps a closed-form solution — and multiplicative structures $(A_1B_1x)\odot\sigma(A_2B_2x)$ are all worth considering as alternative dictionaries. But the experiment below indicates that **the dictionary is a second-order question; the level of the objective is the first-order one**.)

#### Optimizing the composite map directly: the only thing that worked

If the level is wrong, raise the objective to the right level — jointly over **all 36 blocks**, on an end-to-end divergence:

$$\min_{\{A_\ell,B_\ell\}}\ \mathbb{E}_{x\sim\mu}\Big[\mathrm{KL}\big(F(x)\,\|\,\tilde F(x)\big)+\mathrm{CE}+\alpha\sum_\ell\frac{\|h^S_\ell-h^T_\ell\|^2}{\|h^T_\ell\|^2}\Big]$$

The first two terms live at the level of the composite map; the third (following LRC's activation-clone idea) gives every layer an anchor, since a single output-level signal cannot tell layer $\ell$ what to do. The essential point is that this is a **joint** optimization, so the error-cancellation structure is permitted — even encouraged — rather than destroyed as it was by layer-wise reconstruction.

Starting from the ASVD solution, 200 steps on a single GPU (~3.3B effective tokens, 55 minutes):

| step | val loss |
|---|---|
| 0 | 8.4834 |
| 25 | 7.6570 |
| 50 | 7.1616 |
| 100 | 6.3960 |
| 150 | 6.1452 |
| **200** | **6.1208** |

Viewed together, **the level of the objective is the only variable that ever mattered**:

| method | level of the objective | val loss |
|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | per-matrix **weight** error | 18.65 |
| ASVD (closed-form optimal in its class) | per-matrix **output** error | 8.50 |
| layer-wise reconstruction (teacher forcing) | per-**block** output error | 12.75 |
| layer-wise reconstruction (sequential) | per-block, student inputs | 10.54 |
| **end-to-end distillation (200 steps)** | **composite map** | **6.12** |

Moving from "per-block approximation" to "composite-map approximation" is worth **2.36 nats**, while every attempt that stayed at a lower level (more rank, scalar compensation, block 0 full-rank, layer-wise optimization) was **negative**.

**Ablation: the level matters an order of magnitude more than the signal source.** Dropping each loss term in turn, same 200 steps:

| configuration | KL | CE | act clone | val loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full | 1.0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | **6.121** |
| no act clone | 1.0 | 1.0 | 0 | 6.252 (−0.131) |
| no KL | 0 | 1.0 | 1.0 | 6.150 (−0.029) |

Both terms contribute, and both contributions are small. Notably **KL is worth only 0.029** — the KL term falls the most in absolute terms during training (6.32 → 4.13), which invites the inference that it is the main driver, but a large drop in a loss term does not mean a contribution to val loss. CE + activation clone alone reaches 6.150.

Conversely activation clone is worth 0.131 even though its own MSE barely moves (0.790 → 0.758) — not a contradiction: it acts as a regularizer constraining the optimization path, and does not need to be minimized itself.

**So the real conclusion is this**: essentially all of the 2.36-nat improvement comes from **jointly optimizing the 36 layers**, not from any particular teacher signal. The three configurations differ by at most 0.13, while all of them beat layer-wise reconstruction (10.54) by 4.4. **Raising the objective from "per-block" to "composite map" is worth 4.4 nats; swapping teacher signals at the correct level is worth 0.1.** That is the quantitative expression of the level being the first-order issue and the choice of signal a second-order one.

(A practical corollary: KL requires the teacher's full logits, costing roughly 30% of step time in chunked computation, for 0.029 — dropping it is the better trade for longer runs.)

**Extending to 1000 steps: the flattening was indeed an artifact.** Using the configuration the ablation selected (drop KL, keep CE + activation clone), 4.4 hours on one GPU:

| 100 | 6.6354 | −1.848 |

The apparent flattening at 200 steps (6.145 → 6.121) really was OneCycleLR decaying to zero — given enough steps it continues down to **4.5341**. This time convergence is genuine: the last 100 steps move it by 0.0002.

**4.53 crosses two meaningful thresholds:**

1. **Below the noise control's 5.20** — the level produced by injecting 38% random noise into the teacher's final hidden state. The student's hidden-state fidelity is now better than that benchmark, so it is no longer in the "representation has collapsed" regime.
2. **Close to the from-scratch 1.7B at step 500** (4.6368 @ 1.05B tokens), while the distillation used only **65.5M tokens** (1000 steps × 8 accum × 8192) — **1/16 of the data**.

The complete comparison then becomes:

| method | level of the objective | tokens | val loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| plain SVD | per-matrix **weight** error | 0 | 18.65 |
| ASVD (closed-form optimal in its class) | per-matrix **output** error | 0 | 8.50 |

One caveat must be attached: this cannot be read as "distillation is 16× more efficient than pre-training." The distillation's 65.5M tokens come **under teacher supervision**, and its ceiling is bounded by the teacher (val loss 2.33); self-supervised pre-training has no such ceiling. They are different processes, comparable only along the single axis of "how much data to reach a given loss."

A gap of 2.2 remains between 4.53 and the teacher's 2.33. Whether that gap is the low-rank capacity ceiling or merely the limit of a 1000-step / 65.5M-token budget is not yet separated — the curve is completely flat, but flat under this particular LR schedule.

Strictly speaking this is no longer "training-free" but **very cheap recovery training** (55 minutes on one GPU) — which is exactly the shared shape of every successful method in the literature: Sheared LLaMA at 3% of the compute, E³-Pruner at 0.5B tokens, LRC at 20B tokens. "Compress and use as-is" does not hold at 85% compression; "compress plus a tiny amount of recovery" does, and the correct objective for that recovery must be the composite map, not the weight matrices.

#### Against LRC: should the teacher be discarded?

LRC (*A Token is Worth over 1,000 Tokens*, NeurIPS 2025) is closely related to this section, with one fundamental design disagreement. Its parameterization is

$$W^S_{m,i}=W^T_{m,i}\,W^P_{m,i},\qquad m\in\{q,k,v,o,\text{up},\text{gate},\text{down}\}$$

**the student's weights are generated by multiplying the frozen teacher weights by a trainable projection matrix**; the only trainable parameters are those projections plus RMSNorm (<1% of the total), and the student weights are never trained directly. It also has an elegant alignment-free trick: teacher and student hidden sizes differ, so ordinary feature distillation needs extra alignment matrices, whereas LRC **reuses the very projection that generated the weights** to project the teacher's activations (since a transformer module's output is a linear combination of its output-projection weights) — at zero extra parameters. With 20B tokens it brings LRC-1.7B to 64.98, beating Qwen3-1.7B's 63.17 trained on 36T tokens.

This pinpoints a choice buried in the LoRD premise. Our setup **discards the original weights entirely** after the SVD, leaving A and B to train freely; LRC **permanently retains the teacher weights as generating factors**. That may be the structural reason our initialization advantage decayed to zero: freely-trained A, B can drift arbitrarily far, with nothing anchoring the teacher's information, whereas LRC's student **cannot drift by construction** — gradients can only adjust how the teacher is projected, never discard its structure. In other words, that is not "a better initialization" but **turning the teacher into a permanent constraint**.

(The paper offers one more relevant data point: hard-pruning 50% of Llama-7B collapses performance from 63.25 to 48.98, confirming that information loss from hard truncation is the central pain point of this family of methods — our block 0 collapse after being squeezed to 1.2% is the extreme version of it.)

**Two points must be stated honestly:**

There are two explanations we currently cannot distinguish:

- **Explanation A (a real capacity bottleneck)**: the rank-384 structure fundamentally limits expressiveness. $AB$ has rank at most 384 while a dense matrix reaches 4096, so certain necessary high-rank transformations can never be learned. This is consistent with our earlier rank sweep — even rank=4096 (more parameters than the original model) still had a zero-training loss of 2.11, indicating per-matrix factorization loses information intrinsically.
- **Explanation B (unreliable extrapolation)**: all 7 data points lie within 7.3B tokens, and extrapolating them to 25B–100B spans more than an order of magnitude. The $E$ term is commonly overestimated in this regime — the real curve may deviate from the power-law form later on.

**Distinguishing these requires actually training past step 12000.** Our fit predicts 2.406 at step 12000; if the measured value comes in materially below that, Explanation B holds.

### Training Speed: An Unexpected Finding

This run took far longer than expected (22.7 days for 12000 steps, versus roughly a week for the same step count on the from-scratch 1.7B). Investigation found two causes:

**Cause 1: `lord_pretrain.py` omitted FA2 and Liger Kernel.** The from-scratch script explicitly passes `attn_implementation="flash_attention_2"` and calls `apply_liger_kernel_to_qwen3()`; the LoRD script does neither, so transformers fell back to SDPA.

This does not affect any of the loss comparisons (SDPA and FA2 are mathematically equivalent; Liger is a numerically equivalent fused kernel), but it does affect speed.

**Cause 2 (the dominant one): the architecture itself.** We benchmarked fwd+bwd step time per configuration (4 layers, seq 8192, rank 384):

| Configuration | ms/step | vs SDPA |
|---|---|---|
| SDPA, plain (current) | 523.5 | 1.000x |
| FA2, plain | 567.5 | 0.922x |
| FA2 + Liger | 823.9 | 0.635x |

**FA2 is actually *slower* here, and Liger slower still.** (This benchmark shared a GPU with the live training job, so the absolute numbers are unreliable; but it does establish that the missing FA2 is not what accounts for 22.7 days.)

The real reason: **low-rank factorization saves parameters and memory, not computational depth.** LoRD runs the Qwen3-8B architecture (36 layers × hidden 4096) whereas the from-scratch 1.7B is 28 layers × hidden 2048. Although $x \to xB^T \to (xB^T)A^T$ has fewer total FLOPs than a dense $xW^T$, both the depth and width of the computation graph are dictated by the 8B architecture. This is an inherent cost of the LoRD approach and worth factoring into the design: **the parameter compression ratio does not translate into a training speedup ratio.**

### Open Questions

1. ~~**Can the initialization improvement penetrate training?**~~ **Answered: no.** See the two-arm controlled experiment above — 10.124 → 0.038 over 500 steps (1.05B tokens). This also explains why the earlier SVD-vs-random comparison appeared to say the opposite: an 11.68 vs 18.61 gap still left 3.41 after 2000 steps, whereas 8.49 vs 18.61 went to zero within 500. The difference is not "whether initialization matters" but the magnitude of the initialization gap: random init destroys the model's entire structure, while the two SVD variants differ only in precision within the same structure.
2. **Is the 2.372 irreducible term a real low-rank bottleneck or an extrapolation artifact?** Requires training past step 12000 to measure. **This is now the most valuable open question** — with initialization shown to be irrelevant, LoRD's success or failure rests entirely on this ceiling.
3. **Is rank=384 the optimal rank?** We know rank=1024 gives a lower initial loss but with more parameters, and rank=4096 still yields 2.11. If the 2.372 ceiling is set by rank, then searching for the optimal rank under a fixed parameter budget (fewer layers in exchange for higher rank?) is far more meaningful than further initialization tuning.
4. **The cost of inheriting the architecture**: LoRD keeps the 8B's 36 layers × 4096, so its body has 26% fewer parameters than the 1.7B yet runs slower. What happens to wall-clock efficiency if the saved parameters are spent on fewer layers or a higher rank instead?

## Related Work

Our experiments are closely related to the following research directions:

### Theoretical Foundations of Knowledge Storage and Extraction

**Allen-Zhu & Li, "Physics of Language Models" series (ICML/ICLR 2024-2025)** provides the closest theoretical framework:
- **Part 3.1 (Knowledge Storage and Extraction)**: Using synthetic biography data, they prove that **without sufficient data augmentation, models store knowledge but cannot extract it**. 5 diverse surface forms improve accuracy from 9.7% to 96.6%. Our permutation method directly implements their recommendation and validates it on a real 1.7B model with real-world knowledge.
- **Part 3.3 (Knowledge Capacity Scaling Laws)**: Quantifies storage capacity at ~2 bits/parameter. Our 1.7B model's theoretical capacity far exceeds the 14K facts needed, confirming the bottleneck is training efficiency and extractability, not storage capacity.

### The Reversal Curse and Permutation Training

**Berglund et al. (2023), "The Reversal Curse"** shows that models trained on "A is B" fail to infer "B is A" (GPT-4: forward 79% vs reverse 33%). Our permutation method is essentially a mitigation — presenting the same knowledge in different contexts and orderings forces bidirectional associations.

**Guo et al. (2024), "Semantic-aware Permutation Training (SPT)"** segments sentences into semantic units and permutes them, bringing reversed question performance close to forward. They permute intra-sentence semantic units; we permute inter-fact ordering within samples — same methodology, different granularity.

### Knowledge Injection Efficiency

**Lu et al. (2024), "Scaling Laws for Fact Memorization"** finds memorizing all Wikidata facts requires 1000B parameters for 100 epochs, with negative exponential returns per epoch. Our permutation method achieves 81.2% storage with only 41M tokens — far exceeding their scaling predictions — by optimizing augmentation strategy rather than brute-force scaling.

**Muennighoff et al. (2023)** and **Hernandez et al. (2022, Anthropic)** demonstrate that simple data repetition has a ceiling (~4 useful epochs) and excessive repetition damages model capabilities. Our permutation method circumvents this by creating genuinely diverse training instances from the same underlying knowledge.

### Low-Rank Compression and Activation-Aware Decomposition

**Hsu et al. (2022), "FWSVD"** first identified the objective mismatch in per-matrix SVD, weighting weights by Fisher information. **Yuan et al. (2023), "ASVD"** proposed diagonal scaling by activation magnitude — the source of our diag mode. **Wang et al. (2024), "SVD-LLM"** used Cholesky whitening to make truncation exactly optimal for the output error — the source of our whiten mode.

These works share a common goal: **inference-time compression** — the factorized model is used directly without further training, so zero-shot fidelity is what matters. LoRD's setting differs: we treat the factorization as a **pre-training initialization**, with many gradient updates available afterward to repair the error. This raises a question that has not been studied: **is the factorization that is optimal for zero-shot fidelity also the optimal initialization for subsequent training?** The two need not coincide — whitening, for instance, could introduce ill-conditioning that slows convergence.

**Xia et al. (2024), "Sheared LLaMA"** used structured pruning plus continued pre-training to compress LLaMA2-7B to 1.3B/2.7B — the same goal as LoRD via a different mechanism (removing entire dimensions vs low-rank factorization). They demonstrated that "compress a large model + continue pre-training" beats from-scratch training, consistent with the ~4× data efficiency gain we observe.

### Novel Contributions of This Work

| Contribution | Prior Gap |
|---|---|
| Validates permutation on real 1.7B model + PopQA | Allen-Zhu uses synthetic data on small models |
| Quantifies storage-retrieval gap (81.2% vs 43.6%) | Prior work only qualitative |
| Proves base model general capability determines retrieval efficiency (43.6% vs 85.8%) | No prior systematic comparison |
| 41M targeted tokens > trillions general tokens (for specific knowledge) | No direct comparison existed |
| LoRD: discard the original weights entirely and continue pre-training from $AB$ | ASVD/SVD-LLM target inference-time compression, not a pre-training initialization |
| Optimal diag-ASVD exponent is $\alpha=1.0$, not the published 0.5 | Prior work tuned $\alpha$ for compression quality, not for trainability |

## Conclusions

1. A 1.7B model trained on ~98B tokens reaches val loss ~2.58, consistent with power-law predictions
2. Scaling law can be accurately extrapolated from early data points (within 5B tokens)
3. The model first learns deterministic patterns (subword continuation, punctuation), then semantics and knowledge
4. With unique data and 1 epoch, there is no overfitting — val loss decreases monotonically
5. Document boundaries are the model's greatest prediction challenge, reflecting the fundamental nature of context dependence
6. Knowledge retention strongly correlates with training data exposure: high-frequency knowledge (>100K occurrences) is reliably memorized, while long-tail knowledge (<100 occurrences) is virtually impossible to acquire
7. Compressing Qwen3-8B via low-rank factorization as a pre-training starting point (LoRD) reaches val loss 2.545 at 7.34B tokens, beating the from-scratch run's 25B-token endpoint of 2.576. Peak data efficiency was ~2.65× (step 2000) but has already fallen back to 2.41×
8. LoRD's scaling law is $L(D)=2.438D^{-1.323}+2.372$ — a far steeper exponent than from-scratch's 0.658, but a higher irreducible term (2.372 vs 2.00). **Faster convergence, higher floor**; the two curves are projected to cross near 47B tokens. Whether this is a genuine low-rank capacity bottleneck or an extrapolation artifact remains open
9. The objective of the low-rank factorization is decisive: replacing $\|W-AB\|$ with $\|WX-ABX\|$ drops the zero-training loss from 18.65 to 8.50 (−54%) at identical parameter count. The objective is an order of magnitude bigger lever than rank allocation
10. **But initialization quality does not penetrate training**: in a two-arm controlled run, an 18.61 vs 8.49 gap decayed 99.6% to 0.038 within 500 steps (1.05B tokens). The objective matters enormously for **training-free compression** and barely at all for **continued pre-training** — these two conclusions must be stated separately
11. Parameter compression ratio does not translate into a speed-up: LoRD's body has 26% fewer parameters than the 1.7B, yet each step is *slower*, because it inherits the 8B architecture (36 layers × 4096) — factorization saves parameters and memory, not computational depth
12. **The "level" of the approximation is the only variable that truly matters in compression.** Per-matrix and per-block objectives are simply the wrong level mathematically: the 36 single-block deltas sum to 10.10 (predicting a loss of 12.43 if additive) while the actual full model is 8.50 — a **sublinearity factor of 1.64×** — and the cumulative curve shows increments as negative as **−3.20** (factorizing one more block *lowers* the loss): later layers compensate for earlier ones' errors. A per-layer objective forbids that cancellation degree of freedom, which is why layer-wise optimization came out *worse* (12.75 / 10.54 vs the closed form's 8.50)
13. **Switching to a joint 36-layer optimization of the composite map is the only thing that worked**: 8.50 → 4.53 (1000 steps, 65.5M tokens, 4.4 hours on one GPU). Ablations show the gain comes almost entirely from the joint optimization itself rather than any particular teacher signal (KL is worth 0.029, activation clone 0.131) — i.e. **the level is a first-order issue (worth 4.4 nats) and the signal source a second-order one (worth 0.1 nat)**
14. At 85% compression, "compress and use as-is" does not hold (literature SOTA reaches only PPL 32 even at 60% compression), but "compress plus a tiny amount of recovery training" does — the shared shape of successful methods like Sheared LLaMA (3% of compute), E³-Pruner (0.5B tokens), and LRC (20B tokens)
